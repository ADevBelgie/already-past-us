## The overtake you did not see

Most people meet AI through a free app. In a nine-country survey published by the Reuters Institute in October 2026, about 18% of people had any paid access to an AI tool.[^s1-paid] OpenAI's own figures from February 2026 point the same way: about 50 million paying subscribers out of roughly 900 million weekly ChatGPT users, or about 1 in 18.[^s1-openai] The Reuters Institute notes that free versions are often not the latest models. Free users also do less with them: 11% had used an AI agent for a real task in the past week, against 39% of users with paid access.[^s1-paid]

The public verdict follows. In a Bentley-Gallup poll from May 2026, Americans rated AI worse than people at every task they were asked about, as they had in 2023. The gap is closing slowly: the share saying "worse" fell by 5 to 7 points for driving, financial advice and medical advice.[^s1-gallup]

Professional forecasters were cautious too, and the record now shows by how much. In 2022 the Forecasting Research Institute (FRI) asked superforecasters and subject experts about four AI tests, including whether AI would reach gold at the International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO). Looking back in September 2026, FRI found that the outcomes that actually happened had been given an average probability of 9.7% by superforecasters and 24.6% by experts.[^s1-xpt] Experts expected IMO gold around 2030 and superforecasters around 2035.[^s1-imo-forecast] AI reached it in July 2025.[^s1-imo-2025] A newer FRI panel expected about 14% on a hard contest-coding test by the end of 2026; the best score was 53.8% by May.[^s1-leap]

The same record has a second side, and it deserves the same weight.

Faster progress on tests has not yet shown up as faster work. In a 2025 randomised trial by METR, 16 experienced open-source developers took 19% longer on tasks when allowed to use AI, yet believed afterwards that it had made them 20% faster.[^s1-metr25] A larger follow-up pointed toward a speedup, but its uncertainty ranges included zero and METR calls it "an unreliable signal".[^s1-metr26] Danish administrative records on earnings and hours rule out effects larger than 2%, two years after ChatGPT launched.[^s1-denmark]

Forecasters also guessed too high in places. A 2025 survey of 421 forecasters expected 88% on the SWE-bench Verified coding test by year end; the best result was 80.9%.[^s1-aidigest] Forecasters and experts expected 16% to 40% of science students to complete a set of three biosecurity test tasks with AI help; a 2025 trial found 5.2%.[^s1-bio] FRI also warns that its own review leans toward finding underestimates. A forecast that was too low is confirmed the moment AI passes it, while one that was too high is confirmed only after its deadline.[^s1-xpt]

The fair reading is narrow. On tasks with a clear score, AI has moved faster than nearly anyone predicted. In paid work, the measured gains are so far small or unclear.

> **Expert note:** The 9.7% and 24.6% figures are averages of the probabilities implied by median forecasts on four questions from the 2022 Existential Risk Persuasion Tournament (MATH, MMLU, QuALITY, IMO gold). Medians can hide more accurate subgroups. For IMO gold alone the implied probabilities were 8.6% (experts) and 2.3% (superforecasters). The July 2025 result was announced by the labs. Google DeepMind's 35/42 was graded and certified by IMO coordinators; OpenAI's equal score was graded by people OpenAI hired. Not every forecast has been blown past. FRI's panel put the METR 80%-success time horizon at 3.4 hours (experts) or 3.5 hours (superforecasters) by end-2026, and the best measured was about 3.1 hours as of May 2026. The coding question (LiveCodeBench Pro Hard) also formally resolves only at end-2026. The AI Digest pool was self-selected and leaned optimistic: half expected human-level AI by 2030.

> **Expert note:** METR's 2025 trial used early-2025 tools, mainly Cursor Pro with Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet, and METR now marks the page as out of date. In the late-2025 follow-up (57 developers, 800+ tasks), 30-50% of developers held back tasks they did not want to do without AI. That selection probably biases the measured speedup downward, which is why METR is redesigning the study. The Danish paper has been revised and retitled. Its 2% bound is measured from ChatGPT's launch, not from each worker's adoption. Its data cover 2023-24 and so predate current models. The Reuters Institute sample is a non-probability online panel of about 2,000 people per country. In it, 10% pay for AI themselves and 9% get access through work or school; the two overlap, giving 18% in all.
