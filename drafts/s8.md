## Report card: how our July brief did

On 10 July 2026 we published *Measuring the End of Measurement*. It argued that AI tests wear out faster than people can replace them, and that they break in sudden jumps. Here is our own marking, four months on.

The direction held. Where we put numbers on the future, we were too cautious. And we described three of our own headline figures wrongly.

| What we said in July | What happened by November 2026 | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Tests lose half their usefulness in about 8 months, a "conservative" estimate. | Scale rebuilt its hard coding test, SWE-bench Pro, repairing or retiring 480 of 731 tasks. On the new version, agents score up to 99.4%.[^s8-swev2] | Right, if anything slow |
| ARC-AGI-3, a puzzle-game test where AI started below 1%, would move in jumps; 30–50% by end-2026 was "merely ambitious". | GPT-6 Astra scored 62.7% on ARC Prize's standard setup by 2 September (99.9% on a setup OpenAI supplied).[^s8-arc3] | Shape right, too cautious |
| The length of task AI can finish was doubling about every 4 months. | METR's data agrees (about 129 days). But its tasks top out near 16 hours, and METR stopped updating the page on 8 September.[^s8-metr] | Right, now uncheckable |
| AI was speeding up AI research by "tens of %". | METR, using private Anthropic data, estimates Anthropic's progress rate rose 1.9x after October 2025; 1.4x across all developers.[^s8-accel] | Roughly right; low for Anthropic |
| Spending discipline at big tech could slow the race. | The four largest cloud firms are on course to spend about $720–740 billion on capital in 2026.[^s8-capex] | Not visible |
| 59.1% was the "standardized" SWE-bench Pro top score, up from about 46% in ten weeks. | It was one model's score (GPT-5.4), so the trend may compare scores posted at different times.[^s8-scale] | Misdescribed |
| 69.2% was a "vendor aggregate". | It was Claude Opus 4.8's own reported score.[^s8-llmstats] | Misdescribed |
| GPT-5.6 Sol "reportedly" scored 92.5% on ARC-AGI-2. | ARC Prize verified it on 9 July.[^s8-arc2] | Misdescribed, undersold |

Our guesses about the future erred in one direction: things came sooner. Our source labels erred in both.

> **Expert note:** Our July dates for a one-working-year task horizon (2029–2032 at METR's 50% threshold) implied a starting point of about 3 hours. That matches METR's 80% horizon for the early Claude Mythos Preview (about 3.1 hours), not the 50% horizon we said we were tracking (about 17.4 hours, which METR describes only as "likely 16 hours or more"). From an 11–17 hour start, the same method gives roughly 2028–2030.[^s8-yearh] We do not count this as a correction: those starting points sit above the range METR says it can measure reliably, and GPT-5.6 Sol's figure depends on how cheating is scored.

> **Expert note:** Two smaller slips. We cited Forrester's figure that firms would defer 25% of planned AI spending to 2027 as if it had happened; it was a forecast.[^s8-forrester] And our July footnote said GPT-5.6 Sol's over-budget ARC-AGI-3 run would count as zero under strict scoring. ARC Prize's policy caps runs at $10,000, but its leaderboard lists over-cap runs with their cost rather than zeroing them.[^s8-cap]

[^s8-swev2]: **Verified**: "SWE-Bench Pro V2: A Cleaner, Harder-to-Game Coding Benchmark", arXiv 2610.24206, submitted 16 October 2026. The original public set had 731 tasks; 480 were repaired or retired (the two combined, so not 480 removed), giving a 642-task V2 split. Frontier agents resolve up to 99.4%; a 51-task Hard split spans 52.9–98.0%. Preprint, not yet peer-reviewed. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24206

[^s8-arc3]: **Verified**: ARC Prize, "OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra on ARC-AGI-3", 3 September 2026 (leaderboard entry 2 September 2026): Astra (max effort) 62.7% on the Standard harness; Astra (high effort) 99.9% on the Provider-Adapter harness. Both are listed as verified; only the Standard score is comparable across providers. The 30–50% range first appeared in the April 2026 strategic brief; the July brief called it "merely ambitious". Launch baseline: the ARC-AGI-3 technical report (arXiv 2603.24621, March 2026) says frontier systems scored below 1%; the often-cited best launch score of 0.37% comes from the leaderboard and press. Leaderboard as of early November 2026. https://arcprize.org/blog/astra ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24621

[^s8-metr]: **Verified**: METR, "Time Horizon 1.1", 29 January 2026: doubling time since 2023 of 130.8 days (95% CI 107–161). METR's time-horizons page: "Measurements above 16 hrs are unreliable with our current task suite"; marked "no longer actively updated", last updated 8 September 2026. https://metr.org/blog/2026-01-29-time-horizon-1-1/ ; https://metr.org/time-horizons/

[^s8-accel]: **Verified**: METR, assessment of AI R&D acceleration at Anthropic, 5 November 2026, an independent analysis of private Anthropic data. Including all developers' models: 1.4x. About 80% of the speed-up is guessed to be due to AI (highly uncertain). Anthropic redacted a majority of the evidence METR found most informative, so this cannot be checked from outside. https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/

[^s8-yearh]: **Our calculation**: from METR's TH1.1 results file (early Claude Mythos Preview: 50% horizon about 17.4 hours point estimate, METR says likely 16 hours or more; 80% horizon about 3.1 hours) and METR's GPT-5.6 Sol summary, 26 June 2026 (50% horizon 11.3 hours when cheating counts as failure, over 270 hours when it counts as success; METR: "we do not consider any of these numbers to represent a robust measurement").
