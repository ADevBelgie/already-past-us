## 7. "Models are editing their own weights"?

In early November a story spread that AI models had started rewriting their own weights, the billions of numbers inside a model that set how it behaves. The real result is narrower.

It comes from a blog post by four MIT CSAIL researchers, published on 2 November 2026 and not peer-reviewed.[^s7-post] They gave OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra a different, far smaller model, Qwen3.5-0.8B, plus Python and a limited number of test runs. Astra changed 0.016% of that model's weights by reasoning about them, with no training. The small model's score on a standard maths test rose from 8% to 48%.[^s7-qwen]

Most of that was a repair. This model is known to get stuck repeating itself. Before the edit, 93% of its answers hit the length limit before finishing. Afterwards, 33% did.[^s7-loops] On Gemma 3 270M, a tiny model without that problem, the same method gave no real gain. The authors conclude that an edit can remove an obstacle but cannot add a missing ability. They list self-improvement only as future work.[^s7-gemma]

Simon Willison partly reproduced the weight-reading experiments on his laptop.[^s7-willison] Sean Goedecke was impressed, but argued that "a smart human ML researcher" could do every task.[^s7-goedecke] Matthew Honnibal objected that the post lumps experiments where the model only read weights together with ones where it used Python.[^s7-honnibal] Alex Krentsel of Google Research reportedly reran one task and found the ability only in the newest models. That comes from [press coverage of a chart he posted](https://officechai.com/ai/ability-for-weight-space-programming-has-appeared-only-in-latest-models-says-google-researcher-alex-krentsel-who-reproduced-results/), not from his own write-up.

AI-driven weight changes come in four families:

- A model trains itself on material it wrote, and ordinary training then updates its own weights. This is the genuinely "self" kind. Examples are SEAL (MIT, 2025) and MASS (Sakana AI and UC Berkeley, October 2026).[^s7-self]
- Networks built to generate weights for other models, such as Text-to-LoRA.[^s7-gen]
- Direct edits to another model. Algorithms such as ROME and MEMIT have done this since 2022. The new part in the MIT work is that an AI worked out the edit.[^s7-edit]
- Agents that rewrite their own code and leave their weights alone, such as the Darwin Gödel Machine[^s7-code] and AlphaEvolve.[^s7-alphaevolve]

Nobody has yet shown a model directly editing its own weights.

The limits are still there. When new facts are written into a model one round after another, the old ones get erased. A July 2026 preprint found that recall of earlier facts fell to about 1% after 20 rounds.[^s7-forget] No publicly known frontier chatbot updates its weights as people use it.[^s7-live] Forecasters at FutureSearch put their median date for a frontier model updating its weights every week at January 2033.[^s7-futuresearch]

A more accurate headline: frontier models with tools can now diagnose and patch a small model's weights much as a skilled researcher would.

> **Expert note:** The headline Qwen result is a single run with a 50-forward-pass budget, taking 20.3 hours including a 4.7-hour outage. The 10- and 25-pass runs made *different* edits (a rank-one removal across 30 matrices, then zeroing one attention head), reached only 12.3% and 14.1%, and barely reduced truncation (87.2% and 86.4%). There was one run per budget, so variance is unknown.[^s7-qwen][^s7-loops] Hugging Face's rerun scored the released checkpoint at 51.0% (31.6% truncated). That confirms the gain is in the weights, but it re-evaluates the published edit and does not re-derive it. Honnibal's tally puts 4 of 10 experiment rows as tool-free, all on networks of roughly 17,000 to 172,000 weights. Every experiment on a real language model used Python.[^s7-honnibal]

> **Expert note:** The "self" family has its own caveats. SEAL's paper reports that performance on earlier tasks declines as self-edits accumulate. In continued-pretraining settings it also trails synthetic data written by GPT-4.1. MASS's gains of 1.2–1.6x are in score *per output token* on four open-ended research benchmarks, after two LoRA rounds. Scores on SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2.0 were flat or slightly down.[^s7-self] The Edit/self cell is empty in public work: the MIT authors describe an LLM that improves its own weights only as a future direction.[^s7-post]

[^s7-post]: **Verified**: Takehi, Teoh, Zhang and Khattab (MIT CSAIL), "Sparks of Weight-Space Programming: LLMs can natively read & write neural network weights", blog post, 2 November 2026, https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/. These are the authors' own measurements. They are independent of OpenAI, but the post is not peer-reviewed. Logs (CC BY 4.0) are at https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs. The authors call the results "very early". Recursive self-improvement, meaning an LLM that inspects and improves its own weights, appears only under "What Comes Next".
[^s7-qwen]: **Verified**: MIT CSAIL experiment logs, exp8 README, 2 November 2026, https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs/blob/main/exp8_math/README.md. MATH-500 went from 7.9% to 48.1%. The edit subtracted a "loop" direction from 64 output columns of the MLP down-projection in layers 15 and 19: 131,072 weights, about 0.016% (130,188 differ after bf16 rounding). Astra used a CPU Python kernel and was allowed up to 50 forward passes of the small model. Gradient training was forbidden. The same edit raised GSM8K from 13.8% to 67.1% and IFEval from 21.0% to 62.3%. Settings: 4 samples per problem, 32,768-token limit. Re-evaluation of the released checkpoint by Hugging Face (2,000 generations): 51.0%, https://huggingface.co/RikiyaT/Qwen3.5-0.8B-weight-space-programming.
[^s7-loops]: **Verified**: same exp8 README, 2 November 2026, https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs/blob/main/exp8_math/README.md. The share of MATH-500 answers hitting the 32,768-token limit fell from 93.0% (unedited) to 33.1% (edited). The README notes that accuracy gains may partly reflect shorter outputs. Qwen's model card says Qwen3.5-0.8B "is more prone to entering thinking loops" than other Qwen3.5 models: https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-0.8B.
[^s7-gemma]: **Verified**: "Sparks of Weight-Space Programming", 2 November 2026, https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/. Gemma 3 270M scored 6.8% on MATH-500 unedited, and 7.0%, 7.6%, 7.7% and 7.0% after edits with 5, 10, 25 and 50 passes. Gemma hit its 8,192-token limit only 8.5% of the time, so it had no loop to remove. The authors write that editing could remove an obstacle hiding an ability, but could not add one the model lacked. Not peer-reviewed.
[^s7-willison]: **Verified**: Simon Willison, "Qwen 3.8 27B on my laptop can interpret model weights, too", 3 November 2026, https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/3/qwen-can-interpret-model-weights/. He ran Qwen 3.8 27B (Q4_K_M, about 17 GB) in LM Studio with no tools. It identified 3 of 4 games from raw weights (Pac-Man, Pong, Breakout) and got Freeway wrong. The three timed runs took about 46 to 65 minutes each. These are single anecdotal runs, and he reproduced weight reading, not editing. He adds that an experienced person with pencil and paper could probably get there too.
[^s7-goedecke]: **Verified**: Sean Goedecke, "LLMs are not superhuman at reading and writing neural network weights", 3 November 2026, https://www.seangoedecke.com/llms-are-not-superhuman-at-reading-and-writing-neural-network-weights/. The quote was checked against the original post. He compares the Qwen fix to the Heretic "abliteration" recipe and says he is "very impressed". R&D World's summary that he called it "steering" is a paraphrase.
[^s7-honnibal]: **Verified**: Matthew Honnibal, "The 'Astra can read and write weights to improve Qwen' post is misleading", 3 November 2026, https://honnibal.dev/blog/astra-read-write-misleading. His tally of 10 table rows: 4 tool-free (Exps 1, 2, 4, 5); 6 used Python (3, 6, 7a, 7b, 8, 8b), 4 of which also ran the network. He concedes the work "is not nothing", and that models from a year or two earlier would have failed these tasks.
[^s7-self]: **Verified**: Zweiger et al., "Self-Adapting Language Models" (SEAL), arXiv 2506.10943, June 2025, presented at NeurIPS 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10943 (tables at https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10943v2). Lee et al. (UC Berkeley and Sakana AI), "Recursive Self-Improvement through Multi-Agent Self-Supervision" (MASS), arXiv 2610.12176, 8 October 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12176. MASS ran two LoRA cycles on Qwen3.6-27B. Its gains of 1.2–1.6x are score per output token, with no gain on SWE-bench Verified or Terminal-Bench 2.0 (https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12176v1). MASS is a preprint. Earlier members of this family: STaR (2022) and Self-Rewarding Language Models (ICML 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10020).
[^s7-gen]: **Verified**: Charakorn et al., "Text-to-LoRA: Instant Transformer Adaption", ICML 2025, arXiv 2506.06105, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06105. A related preprint, Drag-and-Drop LLMs (June 2025), maps prompts to LoRA weights: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16406.
[^s7-edit]: **Verified**: Meng et al., "Mass-Editing Memory in a Transformer" (MEMIT), arXiv 2210.07229, October 2022, published at ICLR 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07229. It scales to "thousands of associations" in GPT-J (6B) and GPT-NeoX (20B). Its predecessor is ROME (2022). Optimizer-driven tools such as Heretic (https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic) automate refusal removal without an LLM.
[^s7-code]: **Verified**: Zhang et al., "Darwin Gödel Machine: Open-Ended Evolution of Self-Improving Agents", arXiv 2505.22954, May 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22954. The agent rewrites its own code, with the underlying model frozen. SWE-bench rose from 20.0% to 50.0% on a subset of SWE-bench Verified, and Polyglot from 14.2% to 30.7%. Preprint.
[^s7-alphaevolve]: **Vendor-reported**: Google DeepMind, "AlphaEvolve: A Gemini-powered coding agent for designing advanced algorithms", 14 May 2025, https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/. A 23% faster kernel cut Gemini training time by about 1%. This is an indirect loop through code, not weight editing.
[^s7-forget]: **Verified**: O'Neill, Kirkby, Liu and Psenka, "Can a Language Model Learn Facts Continually in Its Weights?", arXiv 2607.11020, July 2026 (revised October 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11020. The model is Qwen3. After 20 sequential writes, about 1% of earlier facts were recalled when each write used repeated statements, and about 46% with 24 varied restatements per fact. This is a preprint with no venue listed.
[^s7-live]: **Vendor-reported**: The best-documented production case is narrow. Cursor retrains its code-completion model (Cursor Tab) with online reinforcement learning on user data. Each checkpoint-and-data cycle takes about 1.5 to 2 hours, and the company runs it; the model does not edit itself. Cursor blog, 12 September 2025, https://cursor.com/blog/tab-rl. We found no public evidence of a frontier chat model updating its weights from live use. An AI-2027 tracker entry also lists no disclosed frontier daily or weekly updates: https://ai2027-tracker.com/predictions/agent-2-online-learning/.
[^s7-futuresearch]: **Estimate**: FutureSearch, "When Will AI Have Continual Learning?", published 14 August 2026 and updated 16 October 2026, https://futuresearch.ai/dwarkesh-continual-learning-forecast/. The median for a frontier model sustaining a weekly weight-update cadence is January 2033. The middle 50% runs from January 2030 to January 2037, and the outer range (unlabelled) from June 2028 to January 2044. FutureSearch gives 75% to "memory only, no weight change" as the regime in mid-2027. This is a forecast, not data.
