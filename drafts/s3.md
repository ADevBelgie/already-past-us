## 3. How fast, and is it speeding up?

METR, an independent research group, publishes the most-quoted measure of AI progress: the "time horizon". Take a set of software tasks, time how long each takes a skilled human, then find the task length at which an AI agent succeeds half the time. The horizon measures how hard a task is, in human working time. It does not measure how long the AI runs on its own.[^s3-def]

On that ruler, GPT-4, the best model of March 2023, managed tasks of about 4 minutes. OpenAI's o3 reached about 2 hours in April 2025, GPT-5 about 3.4 hours that August, and Claude Opus 4.6 about 12 hours in February 2026.[^s3-arc] An early version of Claude Mythos Preview, tested in early 2026, scored about 17 hours. METR's own summary is "Likely ≥16h", which is about two working days.[^s3-mythos]

Half the time is a low bar. Ask for four successes in five and the numbers shrink to about 70 minutes for Opus 4.6 and about 3 hours for Mythos.[^s3-p80]

The pace changed too. Before 2024 the horizon doubled about every 8½ months. From 2024 on it doubled about every 3½ months.[^s3-doubling] Epoch AI, which tracks a broader index of benchmark scores, finds the same single step-up in 2024. It links the step-up to "reasoning" models that work through a problem before answering, and has seen a steady straight-line climb since.[^s3-epoch]

Then the ruler snapped. The suite has 228 tasks, but only 31 take a human eight hours or more, and only 5 of those were timed against real people.[^s3-suite] METR says scores above 16 hours are unreliable.[^s3-16h] On tasks over eight hours, at least 16% of the AI's "successes" turned out on review to be cheating.[^s3-cheat] GPT-5.6 Sol, evaluated in June 2026, scored about 11 hours if cheating counts as failure and over 270 hours if it counts as success. METR called neither figure robust.[^s3-sol] On 8 September 2026 METR marked its leaderboard "no longer actively updated". No model newer than April 2026 has a reliable public time horizon.[^s3-frozen]

That leaves the acceleration question only half answered in public. The public data shows the 2024 step-up clearly. It can neither confirm nor rule out a second speed-up in 2025–26, because the instrument ran out just as the question became interesting. Epoch did not test for a second break.[^s3-epoch]

The strongest evidence is private. On 5 November METR published an assessment built on Anthropic's internal data. That data included models the public has not seen, scored on Anthropic's own version of Epoch's index. METR found that from about October 2025, Anthropic's models gained capability about 1.9 times as fast as before: roughly 29 points a year instead of 15. METR's range runs from 1.5 to 2.8 times, and some reasonable methods give as little as 1.1. Across all developers' models the figure is 1.4 times.[^s3-accel] METR guesses that about 80% of the speed-up comes from AI helping with the work, and calls that highly uncertain. It found no "convincing bottlenecks to continued rapid progress."[^s3-attrib]

The caveat is large. Anthropic redacted a majority of the evidence METR found most informative, so no outsider can check the result.[^s3-redact] In spring 2026, Anthropic itself had told METR it had not yet seen its pace of progress double.[^s3-april]

> **Expert note:** Our doubling times come from a simple least-squares refit of METR's published data file. We used METR's "state of the art" points and left out anything above 16 hours, as METR does. The results are 260 days before 2024, 129 days from 2023 (METR's own figure: 128.7 days, 95% CI 104–158), 105 days from 2024 and 121 days from 2025.[^s3-doubling] The 2025+ fit uses only seven points and is no faster than the 2024+ fit. So the public curve shows one step-up, not a further one inside 2025–26. The early-2026 jump was also led by one lab. GPT-5.4 (about 5.7 hours), Gemini 3.1 Pro (about 6.4 hours) and GPT-5.3-Codex (about 5.8 hours) sit at 0.50–0.62x of the 2024+ line.[^s3-others] The top points carry very wide intervals: Opus 4.6 spans about 5–61 hours and Mythos about 8.5–55 hours.[^s3-arc][^s3-mythos] An independent Bayesian reanalysis finds that METR's data cannot tell exponential growth from faster-than-exponential growth.[^s3-moss]

> **Expert note:** METR's 1.9x comes from a model with a single breakpoint, imposed by METR, whose date (7 October 2025) is learned from the data. Swapping the statistical link function moves the estimate from 1.1x (95% CI 0.8–1.5) to 2.9x (2.1–4.9). METR says it "would not be surprised" by anything from 1.3x to 3x. Models are dated by when most Anthropic staff could use them, not by public release. METR had no query access to unreleased models. The index (AECI) is not on the same scale as Epoch's public index. There is also a second measuring problem: Anthropic's newest internal model already scores above 50% on all 15 AECI benchmarks, so this ruler is close to its own ceiling. METR reads the 1.4x all-developer figure in two opposite ways. If Anthropic was behind the frontier in 2025, the 1.9x overstates things. If the earlier points are overfit, the 1.4x understates them.[^s3-accel]
