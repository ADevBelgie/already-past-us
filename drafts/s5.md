## 5. Why "human-level" is the wrong question

"Artificial general intelligence", or AGI, is usually explained as AI that matches people. The main definitions disagree about what to match, so each sets a different test.

<!-- CHART: s5-definitions | Small styled table: four AGI definitions (OpenAI charter, Google DeepMind Levels of AGI, Hendrycks et al., ARC Prize), what each one tests, and where AI stands on it, each row with a provenance chip linking to its footnote | none (values inline, from footnotes s5-charter, s5-levels, s5-hendrycks, s5-arc) -->

| Definition | What it tests | Where AI stands |
|---|---|---|
| OpenAI charter (2018): systems that "outperform humans at most economically valuable work" | Economic output | No measurable test set[^s5-charter] |
| Google DeepMind, "Levels of AGI" (2023) | Percentile of skilled adults, narrow or general | Narrow superhuman AI exists (AlphaFold); general chatbots were rated "Emerging", the lowest level, in 2023[^s5-levels] |
| Hendrycks et al., "A Definition of AGI" (2025) | Breadth across ten areas of thinking, against a well-educated adult | GPT-5: 57% overall, 10/10 in maths, 0/10 in storing long-term memories[^s5-hendrycks] |
| ARC Prize | How efficiently it learns new skills, against people | GPT-6 Astra: 62.7% on the standard version of the newest test; ARC Prize is "not claiming that it is AGI"[^s5-arc] |

The one place the word had a hard meaning was a contract. Microsoft's deal with OpenAI tied payments to OpenAI reaching AGI. In October 2025 the firms gave that call to an independent expert panel.[^s5-panel] On 27 April 2026 they changed the deal again. OpenAI's payments to Microsoft now run through 2030 "independent of OpenAI's technology progress", and the announcement does not mention AGI at all. Simon Willison and The Verge read this as the end of the AGI clause.[^s5-clause]

The people building AI contradict each other. In August 2025 OpenAI's Sam Altman called AGI "not a super useful term". A year later he told TIME that OpenAI would have an internal system he would call AGI by the end of 2026.[^s5-altman] At GPT-6 Astra's launch on 3 September 2026, OpenAI president Greg Brockman closed with "Welcome to the AGI era."[^s5-brockman] Three days later Nvidia's Jensen Huang congratulated OpenAI and posted "AGI has arrived".[^s5-huang] The researcher Andrej Karpathy had said in October 2025 that it was still about a decade away.[^s5-karpathy]

A more useful question is narrower: which task, against which people? Some yardsticks already ask it. METR's time horizon (section 3) measures how long a software task, in skilled-human time, an AI can finish half the time. GDPval has industry experts blind-grade AI work against professionals' work on 1,320 tasks from 44 occupations. In September 2025 the best model was rated as good or better on 47.6% of tasks. OpenAI reports 84.9% for GPT-5.5 in April 2026, a figure nobody outside OpenAI has checked.[^s5-gdpval] The Remote Labor Index (section 0) uses real paid freelance projects, and there the best agent still fails about four in five.[^s5-rli]

Each of these still uses people as the ruler: human working time, experts' deliverables, freelancers' work. The DeepMind levels and the Hendrycks score are human comparisons too, and the Hendrycks authors advise reporting the full profile, not the single number.[^s5-hendrycks] Comparing AI with people remains useful. What misleads is folding every comparison into one finish line. This briefing measures human-relative, task by task.

> **Expert note:** Each better yardstick has limits. GDPval tasks are one-shot and precisely specified, with no back-and-forth and no physical work. In the GDPval paper's own analysis, GPT-5 looked about 90 times faster than experts, but with a "try once, then fix it yourself" workflow it was only about 1.12 times faster and 1.18 times cheaper.[^s5-gdpval] On the Remote Labor Index, the Center for AI Safety found that AI judges overstated the newest models' success by about 2.3 to 2.9 times compared with human graders.[^s5-cais] ARC-AGI-3 depends heavily on setup: Astra's 99.9% came from a harness OpenAI supplied, against 62.7% on ARC Prize's standard harness.[^s5-arc] METR warns that a 50% horizon is not a safe threshold for handing work over (section 3).

> **Expert note:** On the contract, 2024 press reports said the AGI trigger was tied to about $100 billion in profit, which neither company confirmed.[^s5-profit] Whether the expert panel survives the April 2026 amendment is unclear; Microsoft's post is silent on it. On the quotes: Altman's year-end prediction is TIME's paraphrase, not his words. Brockman's longer remarks (roughly, that it is not unreasonable to feel the AGI era has begun) reach us only as paraphrase in press coverage. Huang's post was not tied to Astra's ARC result. Karpathy's own definition is economic ("any economically valuable task at human performance or better"), and "a decade away" is the episode title; he calls this "the decade of agents". Anthropic's Dario Amodei avoids the word, calling it "an imprecise term" with "sci-fi baggage and hype".[^s5-amodei]

[^s5-charter]: **Vendor-reported.** OpenAI Charter, 9 April 2018, still live in 2026: AGI means "highly autonomous systems that outperform humans at most economically valuable work". The charter never puts "most economically valuable work" into numbers. https://openai.com/charter/

[^s5-levels]: **Verified.** Morris et al. (Google DeepMind), "Levels of AGI for Operationalizing Progress on the Path to AGI", arXiv 2311.02462, v1 4 November 2023, ICML 2024, v5 24 September 2025. Level 1 Emerging: equal to or somewhat better than an unskilled human; Level 2 Competent: at least 50th percentile of skilled adults; Level 3 Expert: 90th; Level 4 Exceptional (formerly Virtuoso): 99th; Level 5 Superhuman: outperforms 100% of humans. Narrow Level 5 examples: AlphaFold, AlphaZero, Stockfish. General Level 1 examples: ChatGPT, Bard, Llama 2, Gemini. The placement is the authors' 2023 assessment, restated in v5, not a measurement. https://arxiv.org/html/2311.02462v5

[^s5-hendrycks]: **Verified.** Hendrycks et al. (33 authors), "A Definition of AGI", arXiv 2510.18212, v3 3 December 2025, Table 1. Definition: "an AI that can match or exceed the cognitive versatility and proficiency of a well-educated adult". Ten domains, 10 points each. GPT-5 (Auto mode) 57%: knowledge 9, reading and writing 10, maths 10, on-the-spot reasoning 7, working memory 4, long-term memory storage 0, long-term memory retrieval 4, visual 4, auditory 6, speed 3.
