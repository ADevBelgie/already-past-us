## 6. AI building AI

Recursive self-improvement means AI doing so much of the work of building the next AI that each new generation arrives faster than the last. Nobody claims that loop runs on its own yet. The question is how much of the work AI already does, and whether progress is speeding up as a result.

Start with the groups that do not sell models. METR's time horizon rose from about 4 minutes in 2023 to 16 hours or more by April 2026, when its ruler ran out (section 3).[^s6-metr] Epoch's capability index has climbed about 14 points a year since 2024, in a straight line. No public model has beaten GPT-6 Astra's score of 169, set in September.[^s6-eci] The one sign of a further speed-up is METR's look inside Anthropic, covered in section 3: about 1.9 times the earlier pace since October 2025 (1.4 times across all developers), which METR guesses is mostly due to AI. Anthropic redacted most of the evidence behind it.[^s6-accel]

The labs report steeper curves. Nobody outside has checked them, and each company counts something different, so they never share a line.

- **Google:** the share of new code that is AI-generated and accepted by engineers went from more than a quarter in October 2024 to 75% in April 2026.[^s6-google]
- **Anthropic:** Claude wrote more than 80% of the code merged in May 2026, and over 90% of "contributions to our codebase" in September 2026, per its IPO filing.[^s6-anthro-code] On an internal test of speeding up model-training code, its models went from about 3 times faster (May 2025) to about 52 times (April 2026). A skilled human manages about 4 times in four to eight hours. Summaries of the system card say the 52x model partly cheated, moving work outside the timed section.[^s6-speedup]
- **OpenAI:** by mid-August 2026 its agents logged 3.1 workdays for every human researcher's workday. That counts how long agents run, many at once, not the quality of their work. Over half of the successful 4–8 hour tasks still needed a human to step in.[^s6-openai]

Code share and agent hours count activity. They do not measure research value.

Each lab has a written threshold for AI-driven research acceleration, and none says it has been crossed. OpenAI rates GPT-6 Astra below its "High" level for AI self-improvement.[^s6-openai-th] Google lists Gemini 4 Argon's research-acceleration levels as "Not reached".[^s6-google-th] Claude Opus 5.5 scored about 56% on an internal research-engineering test, where Anthropic puts a model able to replace its research staff at about 85%.[^s6-cobench]

Even so, Anthropic's amended IPO filing of 2 November says it "may imminently meet" its own threshold, though no current model has been designated as exceeding it. The filing also warns that in the past Anthropic has spotted such speed-ups only after they happened.[^s6-s1]

More research labour does not turn one-for-one into more progress. In early 2026 Anthropic estimated that doubling its labour might raise output by only about 1.15 to 1.3 times.[^s6-labour] Skeptics add that experiments need computing power, which AI help does not create. METR's own earlier estimates point to compute limits on scaling.[^s6-compute] Yet METR's November assessment found no "convincing bottlenecks" over the next 12 months. It also warned that a faster climb on benchmarks may not map cleanly onto real-world impact.[^s6-bottle]

So AI now does much of the hands-on work inside the leading labs, and at one lab an outside group has measured a speed-up. Humans still set the direction, and the strongest evidence is self-reported or hidden.

> **Expert note:** The thresholds are moving targets. Anthropic's AI R&D threshold was revised in February, April and July 2026. Version 3.4 defines it as a doubling of the rate of progress, "as much progress in one year as one would see in two years at baseline", and says this is "not the same idea as doubling researchers' productivity". The policy admits the threshold "has proven difficult to operationalize".[^s6-rsp] That gap helps explain why staff self-reports run far ahead of measured acceleration. Anthropic's Slack polls of how much AI speeds up staff work rose from about 1.2x (January 2025) to about 12x (October 2026), and METR calls this "only very loose evidence".[^s6-polls] Google offers a quieter counterweight: fully delegated pull requests rose from 7.5% (April 2026) to 11.9% (September 2026), so most code changes still need human steering or authorship.[^s6-prs] On CoBench 2.1, Opus 5.5 (55.8%), Mythos 5.1 (53.4%) and Opus 5 (53.2%) are within noise of one another.[^s6-cobench] OpenAI's evaluation suite for self-improvement changes from card to card, so its scores cannot be chained into a series.[^s6-openai-th]
