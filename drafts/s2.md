## Cheaper and better, measured fairly

"AI is getting cheaper" only means something if you compare like with like. We use two tests. Cheaper means the same capability for less money. Better means more capability for the same money.

To put models on one ruler, we use the Epoch Capabilities Index, which combines more than 50 benchmarks into a single score where GPT-5 sits at 150.[^s2-eci] For price, we use each company's list price per million tokens (a token is a small chunk of text), counting three input tokens for every output token.[^s2-blend]

**Same capability, less money.** In November 2025 the cheapest model at GPT-5's level was GPT-5 itself, at $3.44 per million tokens. By May 2026 it was Gemini 3 Flash, at $1.13. Today it is GPT-6 Luna or Claude Haiku 5.5, at $0.20. That is about 17 times cheaper in a year.[^s2-cheaper]

**Same money, more capability.** For up to $4 per million tokens, the best score you could buy was 150 a year ago, about 152 six months ago, and 169 today, with GPT-6.1 Sol.[^s2-better]

**A second view: cost per task.** Price per token ignores how many tokens a model burns to reach an answer. Epoch measured what it costs to answer one test question at a fixed accuracy, thinking included, across five benchmarks. That cost falls about 13 times a year. It falls fastest just after a score is first reached and slows over the next two years.[^s2-epoch]

**What these numbers leave out:**
- Token use varies a lot. Artificial Analysis found that one model, Claude Opus 5.5, costs about 10 times more per task at its highest effort setting than at its lowest.[^s2-effort]
- Tokens are not all the same size. Anthropic says its newer models split the same text into about 30% more tokens, so Haiku 5.5 costs a little more than its $0.20 suggests.[^s2-tokenizer]
- We used today's list prices for all three snapshots. Some prices changed along the way, and some are promotions: GPT-5.6 Sol's runs out on 30 November. Adjusting for the prices in force on each date is still to do.[^s2-hist]

**The top end is not getting more expensive on this data.** From November 2025 to May 2026 the most capable model did get pricier, through OpenAI's "Pro" tiers: from $41 to $68 per million tokens. Today the top-scoring model, GPT-6 Astra, costs $20, and the top four models are a statistical tie. One of the four, GPT-6.1 Terra, costs $2.[^s2-top]

Google is doubling the price of Gemini 4 Argon on 1 December 2026, from $2 to $4 per million input tokens and from $10 to $20 per million output tokens. That ends a launch price Google had announced as introductory. Two cheaper Gemini Flash models double on 1 January 2027.[^s2-argon]

What is clearly rising is the total bill. Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet and Meta expect to spend about $720-740 billion on capital investment in 2026.[^s2-capex] Google alone now processes about 32 billion tokens a minute through its model APIs, more than four times a year earlier.[^s2-volume] Each unit of capability keeps getting cheaper, and the world is buying far more units.

> **Expert note:** How the frontier is built. A model counts at a snapshot if Epoch's release date is on or before that date. All three snapshots use one vintage of the ECI file (7 November 2026), so the earlier panels are past frontiers re-measured on today's scale, not what Epoch published at the time. ECI scores carry credible intervals of roughly ±2-3 points, so gaps under about 3 points are noise.[^s2-eci] The headline thresholds are sensitive at the edges: GPT-6.1 Sol sits at exactly $4.00, and a $5 cap lifts the May 2026 value to 156.2 (Gemini 3.1 Pro).[^s2-better] Gemini 4 Argon is coded "Limited access" by Epoch. Some open-weight models with no listed price (Muse Spark Open at 155.4, Beam 1.0 at 150.8) might undercut $0.20 through third-party hosts. A cross-check using Artificial Analysis prices gives about 20 times cheaper, but it relies on one deprecated open model's median third-party host price, so we report the closed-model figure of about 17 times.[^s2-aa-cross] We did not use the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index itself: it changed version several times during the year and its current version does not score 2025 models.[^s2-aa-versions]

> **Expert note:** Other estimates agree on direction, not size. Epoch's decline is about 66% per quarter for a score that has just become state of the art, slowing to about 32% per quarter two years later; Epoch annualises these as roughly 75 times and 4.7 times a year, and the at-debut figure overstates a full year because the rate decays every quarter.[^s2-epoch] An MIT FutureTech paper finds 5 to 10 times a year at a fixed score, but also finds the price of running the top-performing model rising 3 to 18 times a year (April 2024 to November 2025 data). That is a different measure from list price per token, and it is the main published support for "the best AI is getting more expensive".[^s2-gundlach]
