# Scorecard: "Measuring the End of Measurement" (Jul 2026)

Research round r1, compiled 2026-11-07. Each finding shows its confidence and source type. Fact-checker verdicts are listed first. **A "corrected" or "unsupported" verdict overrides the finding.**

## Summary

Scorecard of the 2026-07-10 brief against evidence up to 2026-11-07. Its overall direction held, and the four months since have pushed it further. Benchmarks died faster than its ~8-month half-life. ARC-AGI-3 followed the predicted plateau-then-collapse shape but collapsed much faster than forecast: ARC Prize Verified gives GPT-6 Astra 62.7% on the Standard harness (Sep 2, 2026, $26.1K) and 99.9% on the Provider-Adapter harness ($18.8K). That beats the brief's 30–50%-by-end-of-2026 range months early, and runs under the $10K cap also reached ~50% (GPT-6.1 Sol 52.7% at $7.6K, Gemini 4 Argon 49.8% at $5.8K). The fragmentation of SWE-bench Pro was confirmed even more strongly: Scale rebuilt it as V2 on Sep 22, 2026, repairing or retiring 480 of 731 tasks, and frontier agents score 99.4% at launch. FrontierMath Tier 4 was declared saturated, and at least one officially certified AI perfect score at IMO 2026 was reported. METR's time-horizon instrument effectively broke: the suite is saturated above 16h, GPT-5.6 Sol's 50% horizon ranges from 11.3h to over 270h depending on how cheating is scored, and METR marked its dashboard 'no longer actively updated' on Sep 8, 2026. The brief's title thesis is literally being borne out, but it means watch item 2 (the doubling time) can no longer be cleanly read. The brief was too conservative where it extrapolated: by its own method, a ~11–17h 50% horizon in mid-2026 puts the year-horizon around 2028–2030, not 2029–2032. It also mis-described several of its own headline figures: the 59.1% 'standardized' number belongs to GPT-5.4, the 69.2% 'aggregate' looks like Opus 4.8's single vendor score, the 92.5% ARC-AGI-2 score was ARC Prize-verified rather than 'reportedly', and Forrester's 25% was a forecast, not observed spending. Its capex-discipline premise has not shown up: 2026 big-four capex is reported at ~$720–745B, and the binding constraint so far is chip supply, not discipline. On the author's beliefs: (1) cheaper and better is strongly supported, e.g. ARC-AGI-2 ~90% at $0.44/task versus 84.6% at $13.62/task in February. But the newest METR numbers do not cleanly show faster progress, because the instrument is saturated and confounded by cheating. (2) Superhuman areas: supported in verifiable domains (math, competition problems, ARC efficiency). Astra used fewer actions than the human median on 96% of ARC-AGI-3 levels, though reliability (80% horizon ~3h for Mythos) and cheating are real caveats. (3) The 'AGI = human-level' yardstick: supported by the Astra episode, where Jensen Huang declared 'AGI has arrived' on a 99.9% that ARC Prize did not treat as its comparable score. (4) Recursive self-improvement: real but incremental. OpenAI self-reports reaching its 'automated research intern' goal, and Anthropic's S-1/A says it 'may imminently' meet its RSP threshold. But METR finds Opus 5.5 and GPT-5.6 Sol unlikely to fully automate AI R&D, Anthropic's internal estimate is ~1.5x acceleration, and the much-cited 'weight editing' news was GPT-6 Astra editing a different small model (Qwen3.5-0.8B). The authors explicitly say they did not demonstrate self-improvement. Caveat: the web-search budget ran out before capex and a few deployment items could be checked against company filings. Those rows rely on aggregators and are flagged as such.
