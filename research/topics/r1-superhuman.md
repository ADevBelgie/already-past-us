# Where AI is superhuman (and where it is not)

Research round r1, compiled 2026-11-07. Each finding shows its confidence and source type. Fact-checker verdicts are listed first. **A "corrected" or "unsupported" verdict overrides the finding.**

## Summary

The evidence supports a narrower version of belief (2): AI clearly beats top humans or typical experts on a set of narrow, well-scored tasks, and the set grew a lot from 2025 to 2026. Math: AI went from gold-level 35/42 at IMO 2025 (Google's result was officially certified, OpenAI's was self-graded) to two officially graded perfect 42/42 scores at IMO 2026, from Huawei Celia and Xiaohongshu dots-note-3.0. Only 7 of 666 human contestants scored 42/42. Competitive programming: OpenAI solved 12/12 at the ICPC 2025 World Finals, while the best human team solved 11. OpenAI placed 6th of 330 humans at IOI 2025. The strongest evidence is that AI is now solving open problems in research math, not just contest problems. In May 2026 an OpenAI model disproved Erdős's 1946 unit-distance conjecture; Noga Alon, Tim Gowers and Thomas Bloom backed the result and it was published as arXiv:2605.20695. There are several other verified Erdős-problem results. On structured multiple-choice expert benchmarks, AI is past the human-expert baseline: GPQA Diamond scores of about 90-96% vs 65-81% for PhD-level validators. Applied areas also show superhuman or better-than-human results: AI weather models (GenCast beat the ECMWF ensemble, ENS, on 97.2% of 1,320 targets; Google DeepMind's hurricane-track model beat NHC's official forecasts in 2025), Waymo's crash rates (95% fewer serious-injury crashes than a human benchmark, Waymo-reported), complex diagnostic vignettes (MAI-DxO 85.5% vs 20% for physicians, under restrictive conditions), and classic games. Forecasting is at parity, not superhuman: FRI says the best AI is statistically indistinguishable from superforecasters (as of July 2026). There is real counter-evidence for the author's framing. Real-world end-to-end work is still mostly sub-human: the best AI completes only about 21% of real freelance projects on Scale's Remote Labor Index. Robots are 2-10x slower than humans and fail at multi-step household tasks. ARC-AGI-3 launched in March 2026 with frontier models under 1% vs humans at 100%. It then jumped to 30-63% (and up to about 97-99% in other configurations) by Sept-Oct 2026, but the official pages conflict on which number is the headline. Many 2026 leaderboard numbers come from aggregators or vendors, so they need checking against the source. For belief (3): the jaggedness itself (perfect IMO scores while still failing many real jobs) is the strongest argument that a single 'human-level' line is not a useful yardstick.

## Findings

## Counter-evidence

- Real work: the best agents complete only ~21% of real freelance projects on Scale's Remote Labor Index (Nov 2026), even as they ace olympiads. Benchmark superhumanity does not equal job superhumanity.
- Robotics and physical tasks remain clearly sub-human. Robots are 3-10x slower, fail at multi-step household tasks (kitchen cleaning, trash removal, furniture assembly, cooking), and do not generalize to new environments (Epoch AI, Feb 2026).
- ARC-AGI-3 debuted in March 2026 with frontier LLMs at 0.2-0.5% vs humans at 100%. That shows novel interactive learning was a large gap only eight months ago. The late-2026 'near 100%' figures appear to depend on provider-specific harnesses, and the official ARC Prize pages disagree with each other.
- Forecasting is parity, not superhuman. FRI's p-values (0.14-0.41) cannot reject equal accuracy, and the superforecaster baseline dates from 2024.
- Medicine: the headline diagnostic studies (MAI-DxO 85.5% vs 20%) use rare NEJM puzzle cases with physicians denied normal resources. RCTs mostly show AI as an aid (MASAI: non-inferior on interval cancers, not significantly better). Patient outcomes are not yet shown to improve.
- In 2025, AI IMO gold (35/42) was below the best humans: five humans scored perfect. IOI 2025: five humans still beat OpenAI. Only in 2026 did AI match the very top at IMO (tied with 7 humans at 42/42, not exceeding them, since 42 is the maximum).
- Verification problems: OpenAI's October 2025 claim that GPT-5 solved 10 Erdős problems was wrong (literature rediscovery) and was retracted. Some 2026 'AI solutions' were also rediscoveries. Mathematicians warn that many long AI proofs go unchecked.
- Persuasion: the 'superhuman persuasion' result is against ordinary crowdworkers in short online debates. The large Science 2025 study found that persuasion gains came with lower factual accuracy, and real-world impact may be limited.
- Superhuman Go AIs were beaten by amateur humans using adversarial strategies (2023), so narrow superhumanity can be brittle.
- Multiple-choice expert benchmarks (GPQA) are saturated and possibly contaminated. Beating PhDs on a 198-question test is not the same as doing a PhD's work.
- Stanford AI Index 2026 coverage repeatedly cites a 'can win math gold but can't tell time' finding (analog clock reading) and a gap between benchmark performance and real-world reliability. I could not verify the exact clock-reading numbers (search budget exhausted).

## Surprises

- In July 2026 two Chinese industry models (Huawei's Celia and Xiaohongshu/RedNote's dots-note-3.0), not OpenAI or Google, were the ones announcing officially graded perfect IMO scores. Only 7 of 666 human teenagers matched them.
- At the 2025 ICPC World Finals, AI solved a problem (Problem C) that none of the 139 elite university teams could solve.
- An AI model disproved an 80-year-old Erdős geometry conjecture (unit distances, 1946). Noga Alon says he has stopped working on Erdős problems because AI now solves them.
- In a 2026 randomized trial, doctors given an AI assistant still did worse than the AI working alone, by 11.5 percentage points.
- The US National Hurricane Center in 2026 shaped official forecasts to closely match Google DeepMind's AI model, which had lower track errors than NHC's own official forecasts in 2025.
- Waymo reports 95% fewer serious-injury crashes than human drivers over 270M+ driverless miles.
- Despite all this, the best AI agents fail about 4 out of 5 real freelance jobs, and no robot can yet reliably take out the trash from an indoor bin.
- ARC-AGI-3 went from under 1% for every frontier model in March 2026 to tens of percent (or ~97-99% with special harnesses) by October 2026, one of the fastest rises recorded on any benchmark.
