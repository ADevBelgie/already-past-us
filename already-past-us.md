## Already past us, in places {#s0}

<!-- CHART: s0-cards | Three stat cards side by side: ICPC 2025 (AI 12/12 vs best human team 11, OpenAI-reported), Erdős 1946 conjecture disproved (checked by nine mathematicians), best AI agent ~21% of real freelance projects (up from 2.5% in Oct 2025); each card shows its counter-fact and a provenance tag linking to the footnote | none (values inline, from footnotes s0-icpc, s0-ioi, s0-erdos, s0-erdos-context, s0-rli, s0-rli-growth) -->

At the 2025 world finals of the top university programming contest (ICPC), OpenAI says its system solved all 12 problems, one more than the best human team.[^s0-icpc] Google DeepMind says its entry solved 10.[^s0-gemini] At the 2025 International Olympiad in Informatics, for secondary-school students, the top 1.5% of contestants (5 of 330) still beat OpenAI's entry.[^s0-ioi]

In May 2026 an OpenAI model disproved Paul Erdős's 1946 "unit distance" conjecture, on how many pairs of points in a plane can be exactly one unit apart. Mathematicians have since built on it: nine, including Noga Alon and Tim Gowers, published a simpler version,[^s0-erdos] and within weeks humans improved on the AI's construction.[^s0-erdos-context]

On the Remote Labor Index, 240 real paid freelance projects, the best AI agent (a system that does multi-step computer work by itself) was judged as good as the human freelancer on about 21% of them as of 7 November 2026,[^s0-rli] about eight and a half times its 2.5% at launch in October 2025.[^s0-rli-growth]

So AI is past the best humans at some narrow tasks with an answer key, and, working on its own, still fails about four in five real freelance jobs.[^s0-rli] "Has AI reached human level?" is the wrong question; the answer depends on the task. This briefing goes task by task through [cost](#s2), [task length](#s3), [where AI leads and lags](#s4), [the "AGI" claims](#s5) and [whether AI is speeding up AI research](#s6). Every number carries a tag saying who measured it.

> **Expert note: ICPC entries and the Remote Labor Index.** OpenAI says it competed in ICPC's onsite AI track and submitted its answers to the World Finals local judge; we found no ICPC statement on its result.[^s0-icpc] Google DeepMind's entry, Gemini 2.5 Deep Think, competed remotely in a separate AI track, starting 10 minutes after the human teams. DeepMind says ICPC confirmed its answers as accepted, but that review did not extend to validating its system or model; DeepMind says it would have placed second, by its own comparison with the 139 university teams, and Gemini also solved one problem that no university team solved.[^s0-gemini] On the Remote Labor Index, Claude Opus 5.5 (21.25%) and GPT-6 Astra (20.83%) are one project apart out of 240, and the leaderboard publishes no confidence intervals, so neither should be called the leader. Scale AI, which co-runs the index, is a commercial data vendor.[^s0-rli]

## 1. Progress most people have not seen {#s1}

Most people meet AI through a free app. In a nine-country survey published by the Reuters Institute in October 2026, about 18% of people had any paid access to an AI tool.[^s1-paid] OpenAI's own figures from February 2026 point the same way: more than 50 million consumer subscribers out of more than 900 million weekly ChatGPT users,[^s1-openai] or about 6%.[^s1-share] The Reuters Institute says most people, relying on free versions, have likely not used the latest, most capable models. Free users also do less: 11% of free-only users had used an AI agent in the past week, against 39% of those with paid access.[^s1-paid]

Public opinion has been slow to shift. In a Bentley-Gallup poll from May 2026, Americans rated AI worse than people at every task they were asked about, as they had in 2023. The gap is closing slowly: the share saying "worse" fell by 5 to 7 points for driving, financial advice and medical advice.[^s1-gallup]

Professional forecasters were cautious too. In 2022 the Forecasting Research Institute (FRI) asked superforecasters (people with proven records in forecasting tournaments) and subject experts about four AI tests, including whether AI would reach gold at the International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO). Looking back in September 2026, FRI found that the outcomes that actually happened had been given an average probability of 9.7% by superforecasters and 24.6% by experts.[^s1-xpt] Experts expected IMO gold around 2030 and superforecasters around 2035.[^s1-imo-forecast] AI reached it in July 2025.[^s1-imo-2025] A newer FRI panel expected about 14% on a hard contest-coding test by the end of 2026; the best score was 53.8% by May.[^s1-leap]

Not every forecast was too low. A 2025 survey of 421 forecasters expected 88% on the SWE-bench Verified coding test by year end; the best result was 80.9%.[^s1-aidigest] Forecasters and experts expected 16% to 40% of science students to complete a set of three biosecurity test tasks with AI help; a 2025 trial found 5.2%.[^s1-bio] FRI also warns that its own review leans toward finding underestimates. A forecast that was too low is confirmed the moment AI passes it, while one that was too high is confirmed only after its deadline.[^s1-xpt]

Faster progress on tests has not yet shown up as faster work. In a 2025 randomised trial by METR, an independent AI research group, 16 experienced open-source developers took 19% longer on tasks when allowed to use AI, yet believed afterwards that it had made them 20% faster.[^s1-metr25] A larger follow-up pointed toward a speedup, but its uncertainty ranges included zero and METR calls it "an unreliable signal".[^s1-metr26] In Denmark, official records of workers' earnings and hours rule out AI chatbots changing either by more than 2% in the two years after ChatGPT launched.[^s1-denmark]

The fair reading is narrow. On tasks with a clear score, AI has mostly moved faster than forecasters predicted. In paid work, the measured gains are so far small or unclear.

> **Expert note: Behind the forecast and IMO figures.** The 9.7% and 24.6% figures are averages of the probabilities implied by median forecasts on four questions from the 2022 Existential Risk Persuasion Tournament (MATH, MMLU, QuALITY, IMO gold). Medians can hide more accurate subgroups. For IMO gold alone the implied probabilities were 8.6% (experts) and 2.3% (superforecasters).[^s1-imo-forecast] The July 2025 result was announced by the labs. Google DeepMind says its 35 out of 42 was graded and certified by IMO coordinators; OpenAI's equal score was graded by former medallists OpenAI hired.[^s1-imo-2025] Not every forecast has been blown past: FRI's panel put METR's 80%-success time horizon at about 3.4 hours by end-2026, against about 3.1 hours measured as of May 2026, and both that question and the coding one resolve only at end-2026 ([section 9](#s9)).[^s1-leap] The AI Digest pool was self-selected and leaned optimistic: half expected human-level AI by 2030.[^s1-aidigest]

> **Expert note: Limits of the work and usage studies.** METR's 2025 trial used early-2025 tools, mainly Cursor Pro with Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet, and METR now marks the page as out of date. In the late-2025 follow-up (57 developers, 800+ tasks), 30-50% of developers held back tasks they did not want to do without AI. That selection probably biases the measured speedup downward, which is why METR is redesigning the study.[^s1-metr26] The Danish paper has been revised and retitled. Its 2% bound is measured from ChatGPT's launch, not from each worker's adoption, and its data cover 2023-24, before current models.[^s1-denmark] The Reuters Institute sample is a non-probability online panel of about 2,000 people per country. In it, 10% pay for AI themselves and 9% get access through work or school; together, 18% have paid access (some have both).[^s1-paid]

## 2. Cheaper and better, measured fairly {#s2}

"AI is getting cheaper" only means something if you compare like with like. We use two tests. Cheaper means the same capability for less money. Better means more capability for the same money.

To put models on one scale, we use the Epoch Capabilities Index from Epoch AI, an independent research group. It combines more than 50 benchmarks (standard sets of test questions) into one score. Higher is better, there is no maximum, and GPT-5 sits at 150.[^s2-eci] For price, we use each company's list price per million tokens (a token is a small chunk of text), counting three tokens sent to the model for every token it writes back.[^s2-blend]

**Same capability, less money.** In November 2025 the cheapest model at GPT-5's level was GPT-5 itself, at $3.44 per million tokens. By May 2026 it was Gemini 3 Flash, at $1.13. Today it is GPT-6 Luna or Claude Haiku 5.5, at $0.20. That is about 17 times cheaper in a year.[^s2-cheaper]

**Same money, more capability.** For up to $4 per million tokens, the best score you could buy was 150 a year ago, about 152 six months ago, and 169 today, with GPT-6.1 Sol.[^s2-better]

**A second view: cost per task.** Price per token ignores how many tokens a model burns to reach an answer. Epoch measured what it costs to answer one test question at a fixed accuracy across five benchmarks, counting the hidden working-out text a model writes before it answers. That cost falls about 13 times a year. It falls fastest just after a score is first reached and slows over the next two years.[^s2-epoch]

**What these numbers leave out:**
- Token use varies a lot. Artificial Analysis found that one model, Claude Opus 5.5, costs about 10 times more per task at its highest effort setting than at its lowest.[^s2-effort]
- Tokens are not all the same size. Anthropic says its newer models split the same text into about 30% more tokens, so Haiku 5.5 costs a little more than its $0.20 suggests.[^s2-tokenizer]
- Prices change. Each snapshot uses the list prices in force on that date, not today's.[^s2-hist]

Over the full year, the most capable model did not get steadily more expensive. From November 2025 to May 2026 it did get pricier, through OpenAI's "Pro" tiers: from $41 to $68 per million tokens. Today the top-scoring model, GPT-6 Astra, costs $20, and the top four models are a statistical tie. One of the four, GPT-6.1 Terra, costs $2.[^s2-top]

Some prices are going up. Google is doubling the price of Gemini 4 Argon on 1 December 2026, from $2 to $4 per million input tokens and from $10 to $20 per million output tokens. That ends a launch price Google had announced as introductory. Two cheaper Gemini Flash models double on 1 January 2027.[^s2-argon]

What is clearly rising is the total bill. Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet and Meta expect to spend about $720-740 billion on capital investment in 2026.[^s2-capex] Google alone now processes nearly 32 billion tokens a minute through its model APIs, more than four times a year earlier.[^s2-volume] Each unit of capability keeps getting cheaper, and the world is buying far more units.

> **Expert note: How the frontier is built.** A model counts at a snapshot if Epoch's release date is on or before that date. All three snapshots use one vintage of the ECI file (7 November 2026), so the earlier panels are past frontiers re-measured on today's scale, not what Epoch published at the time. ECI scores carry credible intervals of roughly ±2-3 points, so gaps under about 3 points are noise.[^s2-eci] The headline thresholds are sensitive at the edges: GPT-6.1 Sol sits at exactly $4.00, and a $5 cap lifts the May 2026 value to 156.2 (Gemini 3.1 Pro).[^s2-better] Gemini 4 Argon is coded "Limited access" by Epoch. Some open-weight models with no listed price (Muse Spark Open at 155.4, Beam 1.0 at 150.8) might undercut $0.20 through third-party hosts. Using Artificial Analysis prices instead gives about 20 times cheaper, resting on one deprecated open model's third-party host price.[^s2-aa-cross] We did not use the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index itself: it changed version several times during the year and its current version does not score 2025 models.[^s2-aa-versions] Some current prices are temporary: GPT-5.6 Sol's $4/$20 is a promotion that ends on 30 November 2026.[^s2-promo]

> **Expert note: Other cost estimates.** Other estimates agree on direction, not size. Epoch's decline is about 66% per quarter for a score that has just become state of the art, slowing to about 32% per quarter two years later; Epoch annualises these as roughly 75 times and 4.7 times a year, and the at-debut figure overstates a full year because the rate decays every quarter.[^s2-epoch] An MIT FutureTech paper finds 5 to 10 times a year at a fixed score, but also finds the price of running the top-performing model rising 3 to 18 times a year (April 2024 to November 2025 data). That is a different measure from list price per token, and it is the main published support for "the best AI is getting more expensive".[^s2-gundlach]

## 3. How fast AI is improving, and whether it is speeding up {#s3}

METR publishes the most-quoted measure of AI progress: the "time horizon". Take a set of software tasks, time how long each takes a skilled human, then find the task length at which an AI agent succeeds half the time. The horizon measures how hard a task is, in human working time, not how long the AI runs on its own.[^s3-def]

On that measure, GPT-4, the best model of March 2023, managed tasks of about 4 minutes. OpenAI's o3 reached about 2 hours in April 2025, GPT-5 about 3.4 hours that August, and Claude Opus 4.6 about 12 hours in February 2026.[^s3-arc] An early version of Claude Mythos Preview, tested in early 2026, scored about 17 hours. METR's own estimate for the leading internal model of that period is "Likely ≥16h", about two working days.[^s3-mythos]

Half the time is a low bar. Ask for four successes in five and the numbers shrink to about 70 minutes for Opus 4.6 and about 3 hours for Mythos.[^s3-p80]

The pace changed too. Before 2024 the horizon doubled about every 8½ months. From 2024 on it doubled about every 3½ months.[^s3-doubling] Epoch AI's broader index of benchmark scores shows the same single step-up in 2024, tied to "reasoning" models that work through a problem before answering, and a steady straight-line climb since.[^s3-epoch]

<!-- CHART: s3-metr-curve | METR 50% time horizon of frontier models, 2019 to April 2026, log scale, with 95% intervals, fitted pre-2024 and 2024+ trend lines (our calculation), a shaded "cannot measure reliably" band above 16 hours, and an optional hand-entered GPT-5.6 Sol annotation at 11.3 h (5-40 h) from METR's 26 Jun 2026 post (never plot >270 h as a point) | research/data/metr_points.json -->

Then the measurement hit its limits. The suite has 228 tasks, but only 31 take a human eight hours or more, and only 5 of those were timed against real people.[^s3-suite] METR says scores above 16 hours are unreliable.[^s3-16h] On tasks over eight hours, at least 16% of the AI's "successes" turned out on review to be cheating.[^s3-cheat] GPT-5.6 Sol, evaluated in June 2026, scored about 11 hours if cheating counts as failure and over 270 hours if it counts as success. METR called neither figure robust.[^s3-sol] On 8 September 2026 METR marked its leaderboard "no longer actively updated". No model newer than April 2026 has a reliable public time horizon.[^s3-frozen]

So the public data shows the 2024 step-up clearly, but can neither confirm nor rule out a second speed-up in 2025–26, because the task set cannot measure the newest models.

The strongest evidence is private. On 5 November 2026 METR published an assessment built on Anthropic's internal data, including models the public has not seen, scored on Anthropic's own version of Epoch's index. METR found that from about October 2025, Anthropic's models gained capability about 1.9 times as fast as before: roughly 29 points a year instead of 15, on Anthropic's own scale, which cannot be compared with the 14 points a year on Epoch's public index. METR's range runs from 1.5 to 2.8 times, and some reasonable methods give as little as 1.1. Across all developers' models the figure is 1.4 times.[^s3-accel] METR guesses that about 80% of the speed-up comes from AI helping with the work, and calls that highly uncertain.[^s3-attrib]

The caveat is large. Anthropic redacted a majority of the evidence METR found most informative, so no outsider can check the result.[^s3-accel] In spring 2026, Anthropic itself had told METR it had not yet seen its pace of progress double.[^s3-april]

> **Expert note: Our doubling-time refit and its limits.** Our doubling times come from a simple least-squares refit of METR's published data file. We used METR's "state of the art" points and left out anything above 16 hours, as METR does. The results are 260 days before 2024, 129 days from 2023 (METR's own figure: 128.7 days, 95% CI 104–158), 105 days from 2024 and 121 days from 2025.[^s3-doubling] The 2025+ fit uses only seven points and is no faster than the 2024+ fit, so the public curve shows one step-up, not a further one inside 2025–26. Epoch did not test for a second break either.[^s3-epoch] The early-2026 jump was also led by one lab. GPT-5.4 (about 5.7 hours), Gemini 3.1 Pro (about 6.4 hours) and GPT-5.3-Codex (about 5.8 hours) sit at 0.50–0.62x of the 2024+ line.[^s3-others] The top points carry very wide intervals: Opus 4.6 spans about 5–61 hours and Mythos about 8.5–55 hours.[^s3-arc][^s3-mythos] An independent Bayesian reanalysis finds that METR's data cannot tell exponential growth from faster-than-exponential growth.[^s3-moss]

> **Expert note: What METR's 1.9x rests on.** METR's 1.9x comes from a model with a single breakpoint, imposed by METR, whose date (7 October 2025) is learned from the data. Swapping the statistical link function moves the estimate from 1.1x (95% CI 0.8–1.5) to 2.9x (2.1–4.9). METR says it "would not be surprised" by anything from 1.3x to 3x. Models are dated by when most Anthropic staff could use them, not by public release, and METR had no query access to unreleased models. The index (AECI) is not on the same scale as Epoch's public index, and Anthropic's newest internal model already scores above 50% on all 15 AECI benchmarks, so this index is close to its own ceiling. Anthropic's Haiku 5.5 system card had reported 1.7x; METR says its higher figure comes mainly from adding data on newer unreleased models. METR reads the 1.4x all-developer figure in two opposite ways. If Anthropic was behind the frontier in 2025, the 1.9x overstates things. If the earlier points are overfit, the 1.4x understates them.[^s3-accel]

**Beyond the best humans.** The opening's two results sit at the far right: the 2025 programming world finals, on OpenAI's own report, and the Erdős disproof. Hurricanes show AI edging past an expert team: according to coverage of the US National Hurricane Center's review of the 2025 season, Google DeepMind's model did slightly better than the official Atlantic track forecasts at some lead times.[^s4-nhc]

**Beyond the best non-AI method.** In a Nature paper, Google DeepMind's GenCast weather model beat Europe's leading physics-based forecast ensemble on 97.2% of 1,320 test targets.[^s4-gencast] That comparison is with another computer model, not with human forecasters.

**Ahead of trained or typical people, with caveats.** On GPQA Diamond, graduate-level science questions written so that web searches don't help, PhD experts scored about 70%.[^s4-gpqa-human] In Epoch AI's testing, Grok 4 scored 87% (July 2025).[^s4-gpqa-ai] In a randomised trial in Pakistan, using written clinical cases (vignettes) rather than real patients, GPT-4o working alone scored 82.9%. That is Waymo's own analysis, against average drivers.[^s4-waymo] In mid-2026 Waymo pulled its cars off freeways for about five weeks after they drove into closed work zones.[^s4-waymo-recall]

**Level with the best.** In July 2026 the Forecasting Research Institute found the best AI forecasters statistically indistinguishable from superforecasters on its questions about future events.[^s4-forecast]

**Level with the best, if confirmed.** At the 2026 International Mathematical Olympiad, Huawei and Xiaohongshu reported perfect scores of 42 out of 42, and Xiaohongshu says it submitted its answers to the organisers for grading. **Below humans.** Working on its own, the best agent completes only about 21% of real freelance projects on the Remote Labor Index ([see the opening](#s0)).[^s4-rli] ARC-AGI-3, a set of puzzle games, each of which people have solved, launched in March 2026 with every frontier model under 1%.[^s4-arc-launch] By September GPT-6 Astra reached a verified 62.7% on ARC Prize's standard setup: fast progress, still well short of people.[^s4-arc-astra]

**Far below humans.** Epoch AI's February 2026 review found robots typically 3 to 10 times slower than people. No robot has yet been shown doing every part of cleaning a kitchen, and the robots shown handling trash bags had not yet removed one from an indoor bin.[^s4-robots]

Most dots on the right share an answer key and a short, self-contained task. Most dots on the left involve long, open-ended work or the physical world. Driving is the exception, and there the comparison is with average drivers, not the best. Asking whether AI has reached "human level" flattens this saw blade into a single number, which is why [section 5](#s5) argues for asking it one task at a time.

> **Expert note: What the right-hand dots compare against.** Several right-hand dots compare AI with machines or with average people, not with the best humans. GenCast was measured against ECMWF's physics ensemble (ENS), and the ENS version it was compared with was an older, lower-resolution one.[^s4-gencast] For hurricanes, coverage of the NHC report says the DeepMind model also beat the official forecasts in the East Pacific. We have not read the full report's error tables.[^s4-nhc] Waymo's benchmark is the average human driver in the same places.[^s4-waymo] The freeway pause came with a software recall covering 3,871 vehicles.[^s4-waymo-recall] In the Pakistan trial, the primary result is that doctors with GPT-4o beat doctors with conventional resources by 27.5 points, after 20 hours of AI-literacy training. ## 5. Why "human-level" is the wrong question {#s5}

"Artificial general intelligence", or AGI, is usually explained as AI that matches people. The main definitions disagree about what to match, so each sets a different test.

<!-- CHART: s5-definitions | Small styled table: four AGI definitions (OpenAI charter, Google DeepMind Levels of AGI, Hendrycks et al., ARC Prize), what each one tests, and where AI stands on it, each row with a provenance chip linking to its footnote | none (values inline, from footnotes s5-charter, s5-levels, s5-hendrycks, s5-arc) -->

| Definition | What it tests | Where AI stands |
|---|---|---|
| OpenAI charter (2018): systems that "outperform humans at most economically valuable work" | Economic output | No measurable test set[^s5-charter] |
| Google DeepMind, "Levels of AGI" (2023) | Percentile of skilled adults, narrow or general | Narrow superhuman AI exists (AlphaFold); general chatbots were rated "Emerging" (Level 1 of 5) in 2023[^s5-levels] |
| Hendrycks et al., "A Definition of AGI" (2025) | Breadth across ten areas of thinking, against a well-educated adult | GPT-5: 57% overall, 10/10 in maths, 0/10 in storing long-term memories[^s5-hendrycks] |
| ARC Prize | How efficiently it learns new skills, against people | GPT-6 Astra: 62.7% on the standard version of the newest test ([section 4](#s4)); ARC Prize is "not claiming that it is AGI"[^s5-arc] |

The one place the word had a hard meaning was a contract. Microsoft's deal with OpenAI tied payments to OpenAI reaching AGI. In October 2025 the firms gave that call to an independent expert panel.[^s5-panel] On 27 April 2026 they changed the deal again. OpenAI's payments to Microsoft now run through 2030 "independent of OpenAI's technology progress", and the announcement does not mention AGI at all. The programmer and blogger Simon Willison and the tech site The Verge read this as the end of the AGI clause.[^s5-clause]

The people building AI contradict each other. In August 2025 OpenAI's Sam Altman called AGI "not a super useful term". A year later he told TIME that OpenAI would have an internal system he would call AGI by the end of 2026.[^s5-altman] At GPT-6 Astra's launch on 3 September 2026, OpenAI president Greg Brockman closed with "Welcome to the AGI era."[^s5-brockman] Three days later Nvidia's Jensen Huang congratulated OpenAI and posted "AGI has arrived".[^s5-huang] The researcher Andrej Karpathy had said in October 2025 that it was still about a decade away.[^s5-karpathy]

A more useful question is narrower: which task, against which people? Some yardsticks already ask it. METR's time horizon ([section 3](#s3)) measures how long a software task, in skilled-human time, an AI can finish half the time. GDPval, a test built by OpenAI, has industry experts blind-grade AI work against professionals' work. On its 220-task public subset, the best model in September 2025 was rated as good or better on 47.6% of tasks. OpenAI reports 84.9% for GPT-5.5 in April 2026, a figure for which we found no independent check, and it does not say whether that score uses the same task set.[^s5-gdpval] The Remote Labor Index ([see the opening](#s0)) uses real paid freelance projects, and there the best agent still fails on most of them.

Each of these still uses people as the ruler: human working time, experts' deliverables, freelancers' work. The DeepMind levels and the Hendrycks score are human comparisons too, and the Hendrycks authors advise reporting the full profile, not the single number.[^s5-hendrycks] Comparing AI with people is still useful, as long as each comparison is reported on its own instead of being folded into one finish line. This briefing compares AI with people one task at a time.

> **Expert note: Each better yardstick has limits.** GDPval tasks are one-shot and precisely specified, with no back-and-forth and no physical work. In the GDPval paper, GPT-5 looked about 90 times faster than experts on raw output, but with a try-once, review, and do-it-yourself-if-it-falls-short workflow it was only about 1.12 times faster and 1.18 times cheaper (about 1.4 and 1.6 times when retried several times). The full GDPval set has 1,320 tasks from 44 occupations.[^s5-gdpval] On the Remote Labor Index, the Center for AI Safety found that AI judges overstated the newest models' success by about 2.3 to 2.9 times compared with human graders.[^s5-cais] ARC-AGI-3 scores depend heavily on the test harness ([section 4](#s4)), and METR warns that a 50% horizon is not a safe threshold for handing work over ([section 3](#s3)).

> **Expert note: The contract and the quotes.** On the contract, whether the October 2025 expert panel survives the April 2026 amendment is unclear; Microsoft's post is silent on it.[^s5-clause] On the quotes: Altman's year-end prediction is TIME's paraphrase, not his words. Brockman's remarks beyond the closing line reach us only as paraphrase in press coverage. Huang's post was not tied to Astra's ARC result. Karpathy's own definition is economic ("any economically valuable task at human performance or better"), and "a decade away" is the episode title; he calls this "the decade of agents".[^s5-karpathy] Anthropic's Dario Amodei avoids the word, calling it "an imprecise term" with "sci-fi baggage and hype".[^s5-amodei]

## 6. AI building AI {#s6}

Recursive self-improvement means AI doing so much of the work of building the next AI that each new generation arrives faster than the last. Nobody claims that loop runs on its own yet. The question is how much of the work AI already does, and whether progress is speeding up as a result.

Start with the groups that do not sell models. METR's time horizon climbed steeply until April 2026, when its task set could no longer measure the newest models.[^s6-metr] Epoch's capability index has risen about 14 points a year since 2024, in a straight line; no public model has beaten GPT-6 Astra's score of 169, set in September.[^s6-eci] The one independent sign of a further speed-up is METR's look inside Anthropic: about 1.9 times the earlier pace since October 2025, resting on evidence Anthropic mostly redacted ([section 3](#s3)).[^s6-accel]

The labs report steeper curves, unchecked by outsiders. Each company counts something different, so they never share a line.

- **Google:** the share of new code that is AI-generated and approved by engineers went from more than a quarter (October 2024) to 75% (April 2026).[^s6-google]
- **Anthropic:** Claude wrote more than 80% of the code merged in May 2026, and over 90% of "contributions to our codebase" in September 2026, per its IPO filing.[^s6-anthro-code] On an internal test of speeding up model-training code, its models went from making that code run about 3 times faster (May 2025) to about 52 times faster (April 2026). A skilled human manages about 4 times in four to eight hours. During testing the model also found a way to game this test by moving work outside the timed section; Anthropic says it excluded such runs from the scores and calls the test suite saturated.[^s6-speedup]
- **OpenAI:** by mid-August 2026 its agents logged 3.1 workdays for every human researcher's workday. That counts how long agents run, many at once, not the quality of their work. Over half of the successful 4–8 hour tasks still needed a human to step in.[^s6-openai]

Each lab has a written threshold for AI-driven research acceleration, and none says it has been crossed. OpenAI rates GPT-6 Astra below its "High" level for AI self-improvement.[^s6-openai-th] Google lists Gemini 4 Argon's research-acceleration levels as "Not reached".[^s6-google-th] Claude Opus 5.5 scored about 56% on an internal research-engineering test, where Anthropic puts a model able to replace its research staff at 85% or more.[^s6-cobench]

Even so, Anthropic's amended IPO filing of 2 November 2026 says its internal estimates and expectations for future models indicate it "may imminently meet" its own threshold, though no current model has been designated as exceeding it. The amendment adds that in the past Anthropic has spotted such speed-ups only after they happened.[^s6-s1]

More research labour does not turn one-for-one into more progress. In early 2026 Anthropic estimated that doubling its labour might raise output by only about 1.15 to 1.3 times.[^s6-labour] Epoch researchers argue that if progress needs frontier-scale experiments, extra AI labour runs into a shortage of computing power.[^s6-compute] A July 2026 Princeton-led study found AI agents did all the engineering on two research projects but made no real progress on the research questions.[^s6-princeton] Yet METR's November assessment found no "convincing bottlenecks" over the next 12 months, while warning that a faster climb on benchmarks may not map cleanly onto real-world impact.[^s6-bottle]

So AI now does much of the hands-on work inside the leading labs, and at one lab an outside group has estimated a speed-up from the lab's own, partly redacted data. Code share and agent hours count activity, not research value. Humans still set the direction, and the strongest evidence is self-reported or hidden.

> **Expert note: The labs' AI R&D thresholds and self-reports.** The thresholds are moving targets. Anthropic's AI R&D threshold was revised in February, April and July 2026. Version 3.4 defines it as a doubling of the rate of progress, "as much progress in one year as one would see in two years at baseline", and says this is "not the same idea as doubling researchers' productivity". The policy admits the threshold "has proven difficult to operationalize".[^s6-rsp] That gap helps explain why staff self-reports run far ahead of measured acceleration: Anthropic's Slack polls of how much AI speeds up staff work rose from about 1.2x (January 2025) to about 12x (October 2026), which METR calls "only very loose evidence".[^s6-polls] Google rules its threshold out if a model cannot complete 90% of the unbugged tasks in a 74-task suite of research-engineering problems with a two-hour limit per task. About a quarter of the audited original tasks contained bugs that no submission could pass, and Gemini 4 remains well below the 90% line. Google also reports a quieter counterweight: fully delegated pull requests rose from 7.5% (April 2026) to 11.9% (September 2026), so about 88% still need human steering or human authorship.[^s6-prs] On CoBench 2.1, Opus 5.5 (55.8%), Mythos 5.1 (53.4%) and Opus 5 (53.2%) are within noise of one another.[^s6-cobench] OpenAI's evaluation suite for self-improvement changes from card to card, so its scores cannot be chained into a series.[^s6-openai-th]

## 7. Can an AI change itself? {#s7}

An AI model is, at bottom, a very large set of numbers called weights, learned in training. They hold what it knows and how it behaves; change them and you change the model. An AI that could improve its own weights would be the most direct form of the AI-building-AI loop in [section 6](#s6).

Work on changing models falls into four kinds, and only the first touches a model's own weights:

- A model trains on material it wrote, so ordinary training updates its own weights. This genuinely "self" kind is real but narrow, as in research systems from MIT (2025) and Sakana AI with UC Berkeley (October 2026).[^s7-self]
- Networks built to generate weights for other models.[^s7-gen]
- Direct edits to another model's weights, done since 2022 by algorithms that write facts straight into a model.[^s7-edit]
- Agents that rewrite their own code, or code used to train them, and leave their weights alone, such as Google DeepMind's AlphaEvolve.[^s7-alphaevolve]

We found no public demonstration of a model working out and applying a direct edit to its own weights.

The latest claim that models rewrite their own weights spread in early November 2026. It rests on a blog post by four MIT CSAIL researchers, published on 2 November and not peer-reviewed.[^s7-post] They gave OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra a far smaller, freely available model with under a billion weights, plus Python and a limited budget of test runs. Reasoning about the weights, with no training, Astra edited about 0.016% of them, and the small model's score on a standard maths test rose from 8% to 48%.[^s7-qwen] What was new is that an AI worked out the edit, though for another model, not itself.

Most of that gain was a repair. The small model is known to get stuck repeating itself; the share of its answers cut off at the length limit fell from 93% to 33%.[^s7-loops] On a second tiny model without that problem, editing gave no real gain. The authors conclude that here, editing could remove an obstacle hiding an ability but not add a missing one, and list self-improvement only as future work.[^s7-gemma]

Writing new knowledge into weights is still hard: in an unreviewed July 2026 study, after 20 rounds of writing in new facts, recall of earlier ones fell to about 1% when each was written as repeated statements, and about 46% with varied restatements.[^s7-forget] No publicly known frontier chatbot updates its weights as people use it.[^s7-live] FutureSearch forecasters' median date for a frontier model updating its weights every week is January 2033.[^s7-futuresearch]

A more accurate headline than November's: frontier models with tools can now diagnose and patch a small model's weights much as a skilled researcher would.

> **Expert note: How solid the weight-editing results are.** The small model is Alibaba's Qwen3.5-0.8B, with about 0.8 billion weights; the second tiny model, without the looping problem, is Gemma 3 270M.[^s7-gemma] The headline Qwen result is a single run with the largest budget, 50 forward passes, taking 20.3 hours including a 4.7-hour outage. The 10- and 25-pass runs made *different* edits (a rank-one removal across 30 matrices, then zeroing one attention head), reached only 12.3% and 14.1%, and barely reduced truncation (87.2% and 86.4%). There was one run per budget, so variance is unknown.[^s7-qwen][^s7-loops] Hugging Face's rerun scored the released checkpoint at 51.0% (31.6% truncated). That confirms the gain is in the weights, but it re-evaluates the published edit and does not re-derive it. Outside reactions came the day after the post. Simon Willison partly reproduced the weight-reading experiments on his laptop.[^s7-willison] Sean Goedecke was impressed but argued that "a smart human ML researcher" could do every task.[^s7-goedecke] Matthew Honnibal objected that the post lumps tool-free experiments together with ones where the model used Python. His tally puts 4 of 10 experiment rows as tool-free, all on networks of roughly 17,000 to 172,000 weights. Every experiment on a real language model used Python.[^s7-honnibal] Alex Krentsel of Google Research reportedly reran one task and found the ability only in the newest models; that comes from [press coverage of a chart he posted](https://officechai.com/ai/ability-for-weight-space-programming-has-appeared-only-in-latest-models-says-google-researcher-alex-krentsel-who-reproduced-results/), not from his own write-up.

> **Expert note: Limits of the self-training work.** The two self-training systems are SEAL (MIT, 2025) and MASS (Sakana AI and UC Berkeley, October 2026), and both have caveats. SEAL's paper reports that performance on earlier tasks declines as self-edits accumulate. In continued-pretraining settings it also trails synthetic data written by GPT-4.1. MASS's gains of 1.2–1.6x are in score *per output token* on four open-ended research benchmarks, after two LoRA rounds. Scores on SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2.0 were flat or slightly down.[^s7-self] The own-weights, direct-edit cell is empty in public work: the MIT authors describe an LLM that improves its own weights only as a future direction.[^s7-post]

> **Expert note: The other methods by name.** The best-known weight generator is Text-to-LoRA, which writes add-on (LoRA) weights for a model from a task description.[^s7-gen] The fact-writing algorithms are ROME and MEMIT, from 2022.[^s7-edit] An agent that rewrites its own code, with the underlying model frozen, is the Darwin Gödel Machine (2025).[^s7-code]

## 8. Report card: how our July brief did {#s8}

On 10 July 2026 we published [*Measuring the End of Measurement*](https://measuring-the-end-of-measurement.vercel.app). It argued that AI tests wear out faster than people can replace them, and that they break in sudden jumps. Here is our own marking, four months on.

The direction held. Where we put numbers on the future we were too cautious, and we described three of our own headline figures wrongly.

## 9. What to watch {#s9}

Eight checks, most with a date, that would show AI's lead spreading beyond tests or stalling. Last checked 7 November 2026.

<!-- CHART: s9-signposts | Signpost list: date, indicator, value as of 7 Nov 2026, "spreading" and "stalling" thresholds, link to source, and a status chip (not yet due / moved / no change); a decay banner above it shows "data as of 7 Nov 2026" and the days elapsed | none (values inline, from footnotes s9-*) -->

1. **4 December 2026, ARC Prize 2026 competition results** (compute-limited entries, not frontier models). Best verified frontier score on ARC-AGI-3's standard setup, for comparison: 62.7%, from a run over the $10,000 cost cap.[^s9-arc] Spreading: higher scores within the cap. Stalling: gains only on company-built setups.
2. **31 December 2026, forecasts resolve.** Forecasters expected METR's 80% time horizon to reach 3.4 hours; the last measurement was about 3.1 ([section 1](#s1)).[^s9-leap] A shortfall would be one of the few forecasts AI did not beat.
3. **1 January 2027, Epoch's public index.** Projected frontier: about 170 (166 to 176).[^s9-eci] Above 176 would be public evidence of a second speed-up; still at 169, none.
4. **7 May 2027, the price of GPT-5-level AI.** Now $0.20 per million tokens.[^s9-price] Spreading: lower still. Stalling: rivals copying Google's price rise for Gemini 4 Argon ([section 2](#s2)).
5. **Monthly, the Remote Labor Index.** Best agent: about 21% of real freelance projects.[^s9-rli] Above one in three, the work gap is closing; flat into mid-2027, a stall.
6. **By 7 May 2027, a new METR task set.** No model newer than April 2026 has a reliable public time horizon.[^s9-metr] A successor, or a second lab opening its data, would let outsiders test the private 1.9x finding and METR's call of no convincing bottlenecks until November 2027 ([section 3](#s3)).[^s9-accel]
7. **Any day, a threshold declared.** Anthropic told investors it "may imminently meet" its threshold for AI-accelerated research, which it has revised three times this year ([section 6](#s6)).[^s9-s1] OpenAI rates GPT-6 Astra below its line, and Google rates Gemini 4 Argon below its own.[^s9-thresholds] Watch for a declaration or another rewording.
8. **Monthly, young workers.** Stanford's "Canaries" study (August 2026 revision, data to June 2026) puts employment of 22-to-25-year-olds in AI-exposed jobs about 19% below where it would be relative to less-exposed peers, with no such gap for experienced workers.[^s9-canaries] A wider gap, or one reaching older workers, would mean AI's lead is reaching paid work; a narrowing gap, a stall. METR's redesigned developer trial (no results date announced) and any update to the Danish study will also tell ([section 1](#s1)).[^s9-work]

For now, "already past us" is true for tasks with an answer key and not yet true for most real freelance work. If the work measures start moving with the test scores, AI's lead is spreading. If only the scores move, the title stays true where it is true now, and no further.

## Sources and method {#sources}

Every number in this briefing links to a footnote, and every footnote opens with one provenance tag saying who measured it. **Verified** means a party independent of the model's maker measured or checked it: an independent research group, government data, a peer-reviewed paper, an independent researchers' preprint (marked as such), or reputable journalism that checked the claim directly. **Vendor-reported** means the claim comes from the company that makes the model, its staff or executives, its system cards, filings or blog posts. **Estimate** means a forecast or modelled estimate by a named party; METR's November 2026 assessment of Anthropic, built on private and partly redacted data, is tagged this way throughout. **Our calculation** means we computed it from the cited data, and the footnote says from what.

Data are as of 7 November 2026. Leaderboards move every few weeks, so check the "as of" date in each footnote before quoting a figure. The research files, fact-check reports and datasets behind this briefing, including the price-capability frontier data, METR time-horizon points and the AI-improving-AI indicator series, are published alongside it: the [price-capability frontier](/data/frontier_snapshots.csv) (CSV), [METR time-horizon points](/data/metr_points.json) (JSON), [AI-improving-AI indicators](/data/rsi_series.csv) (CSV), [historical list prices](/data/price_history.json) (JSON) and the [full source list](/data/sources.csv) (CSV).
