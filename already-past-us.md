## Already past us, in places {#s0}

<!-- CHART: s0-cards | Three stat cards side by side: ICPC 2025 (AI 12/12 vs best human team 11, OpenAI-reported), Erdős 1946 conjecture disproved (checked by nine mathematicians), best AI agent ~21% of real freelance projects (up from 2.5% in Oct 2025); each card shows its counter-fact and a provenance tag linking to the footnote | none (values inline, from footnotes s0-icpc, s0-ioi, s0-erdos, s0-erdos-context, s0-rli, s0-rli-growth) -->

At the 2025 world finals of the top university programming contest (ICPC), OpenAI says its system solved all 12 problems, one more than the best human team.[^s0-icpc] Google DeepMind says its entry solved 10.[^s0-gemini] But at a different contest, the 2025 International Olympiad in Informatics (IOI) for secondary-school students, the top 1.5% of contestants (5 of 330) still beat OpenAI's entry.[^s0-ioi]

In May 2026 an OpenAI model disproved Paul Erdős's 1946 "unit distance" conjecture, on how many pairs of points in a plane can be exactly one unit apart. Mathematicians have since built on it: nine of them [published a simpler version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20695),[^s0-erdos] and within weeks humans improved on the AI's construction.[^s0-erdos-context]

On the Remote Labor Index, 240 real paid freelance projects, the best AI agent (a system that does multi-step computer work by itself) was judged as good as the human freelancer on about 21% of them as of 7 November 2026,[^s0-rli] about eight and a half times its 2.5% at launch in October 2025.[^s0-rli-growth]

So AI already beats the best humans at some narrow tasks that have a clear right answer. Yet working on its own, it still fails about four in five real freelance jobs.[^s0-rli] "Has AI reached human level?" is the wrong question; the answer depends on the task. This briefing goes task by task through [cost](#s2), [task length](#s3), [where AI leads and lags](#s4), [the "AGI" claims](#s5) and [whether AI is speeding up AI research](#s6).

> **Expert note: ICPC entries and the Remote Labor Index.** OpenAI says it competed in ICPC's onsite AI track and submitted its answers to the World Finals local judge; we found no ICPC statement on its result.[^s0-icpc] Google DeepMind's entry, Gemini 2.5 Deep Think, competed remotely in a separate AI track, starting 10 minutes after the human teams. DeepMind says ICPC confirmed its answers as accepted, but that review did not extend to validating its system or model; DeepMind says it would have placed second, by its own comparison with the 139 university teams, and Gemini also solved one problem that no university team solved.[^s0-gemini] On the Remote Labor Index, Claude Opus 5.5 (21.25%) and GPT-6 Astra (20.83%) are one project apart out of 240, and the leaderboard publishes no confidence intervals, so neither should be called the leader. Scale AI, which co-runs the index, is a commercial data vendor.[^s0-rli]

## 1. Progress most people have not seen {#s1}

Most people only use the free versions of AI apps. A Reuters Institute survey across nine countries, published in October 2026, found that only about 18% of people use a paid AI tool, either paying themselves or through work or school.[^s1-paid] OpenAI's own figures from February 2026 tell the same story: of more than 900 million people using ChatGPT each week, only about 6% (over 50 million) pay for a subscription.[^s1-openai][^s1-share] The Reuters Institute says most people, relying on free versions, have likely not used the latest, most capable models. Paying users also do more: 39% had used an AI agent in the past week, against 11% of free users.[^s1-paid]

Public opinion has been slow to shift. In a Bentley-Gallup poll from May 2026, Americans still rated AI worse than people at every task on the list, just as in 2023. Views are shifting only a little: the share saying AI does worse went from 68% to 61% for driving, from 62% to 57% for medical advice and from 49% to 43% for financial advice.[^s1-gallup]

Forecasters were too cautious as well. In 2022 the Forecasting Research Institute (FRI) asked top forecasters and AI experts about AI milestones, including when AI would win gold at the International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO). They expected around 2030 to 2035;[^s1-imo-forecast] it happened in July 2025.[^s1-imo-2025] A newer FRI panel, asked in late 2025, expected about 14% on a hard coding-contest test by the end of 2026; the best AI had 53.8% by May 2026.[^s1-leap]

Not every forecast was too low. Forecasters overestimated some things too, such as scores on a well-known coding test in 2025 and how much AI would help students with biosecurity tasks.[^s1-aidigest][^s1-bio] FRI also notes that its method spots forecasts that were too low more easily than ones that were too high.[^s1-xpt]

Faster progress on tests has not yet clearly shown up as faster work. The best-known measurement comes from METR, an independent AI research group. In its early-2025 trial, 16 experienced open-source developers took 19% longer on tasks when allowed to use AI, yet believed afterwards that it had made them 20% faster.[^s1-metr25] A larger follow-up in late 2025, with newer tools, pointed toward a speedup, but METR calls the result "an unreliable signal" and is redesigning the study.[^s1-metr26] A Danish study of official pay and hours records from 2023 and 2024, before today's models, rules out AI chatbots changing either by more than 2%.[^s1-denmark] Newer evidence, from 2026, points to gains, but small or narrow ones. At one software company, developers who started using AI coding tools finished about 12% more code changes, in records running to April 2026; it was not a controlled experiment, and more code is not the same as more useful work.[^s1-2xmandate] In mid-2026, executives at nearly 9,000 firms in seven countries estimated that AI had added about 0.2% a year to their productivity growth over the past three years.[^s1-firms]

The fair reading is narrow. On tasks with a clear score, AI has mostly moved faster than forecasters predicted. In paid work, the measured gains are so far small or unclear.

> **Expert note: Behind the forecast and IMO figures.** "Top forecasters" are superforecasters, people with proven records in forecasting tournaments. Across four questions from the 2022 Existential Risk Persuasion Tournament (MATH, MMLU, QuALITY, IMO gold), FRI found that what actually happened had been given an average probability of 9.7% by superforecasters and 24.6% by subject experts.[^s1-xpt] These average the probabilities implied by median forecasts, and medians can hide more accurate subgroups. For IMO gold alone the implied probabilities were 8.6% (experts) and 2.3% (superforecasters).[^s1-imo-forecast] The July 2025 result was announced by the labs. Google DeepMind says its 35 out of 42 was graded and certified by IMO coordinators; OpenAI's equal score was graded by former medallists OpenAI hired.[^s1-imo-2025] Not every forecast has been blown past: FRI's panel put METR's 80%-success time horizon at about 3.4 hours by end-2026, against about 3.1 hours measured as of May 2026, and both that question and the coding one resolve only at end-2026 ([section 9](#s9)).[^s1-leap] The AI Digest pool was self-selected and leaned optimistic: half expected human-level AI by 2030.[^s1-aidigest]

> **Expert note: Forecasts that came in too high.** A survey of 421 forecasters, taken from late November 2024 to January 2025, expected 88% on the SWE-bench Verified coding test by the end of 2025; the best result was 80.9%.[^s1-aidigest] Forecasters and experts expected 16% to 40% of science students to complete a set of three biosecurity test tasks with AI help; a 2025 trial found 5.2%.[^s1-bio] FRI's own review leans toward finding underestimates: a forecast that was too low is confirmed the moment AI passes it, while one that was too high is confirmed only after its deadline.

> **Expert note: Limits of the work and usage studies.** METR's 2025 trial used early-2025 tools, mainly Cursor Pro with Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet, and METR now marks the page as out of date. In the late-2025 follow-up (57 developers, 800+ tasks), 30-50% of developers held back tasks they did not want to do without AI. That selection probably biases the measured speedup downward, which is why METR is redesigning the study.[^s1-metr26] The Danish paper has been revised and retitled. Its 2% bound is measured from ChatGPT's launch, not from each worker's adoption, and its data cover 2023-24, before current models.[^s1-denmark] The 2026 evidence is mostly observational or self-reported. At the company with the doubling target, the estimated effect of adoption depends on the statistical method, the raw doubling of code changes per developer is not the AI effect, and the share of changes reviewed by a human fell from 89% to 68%.[^s1-2xmandate] Microsoft's study of its own early-2026 rollout of Claude Code and GitHub Copilot CLI found adopters merged about 24% more code changes than a modelled comparison group (95% CI +14.5% to +33.7%); it is vendor-run, adopters chose themselves, and quality was not measured.[^s1-msft] The firm figure is coded from executives' category answers, not measured output.[^s1-firms] The Bank of England reads the UK version as about 0.1 to 0.2 percentage points a year since early 2024, and finds productivity in AI-exposed sectors still no higher than its pre-Covid average.[^s1-boe] US workers' self-reported time saved rose from 1.6% to 2.2% of work hours between mid-2024 and mid-2026, but METR's 2025 trial shows such self-reports can point the wrong way.[^s1-stlfed] The Reuters Institute sample is a non-probability online panel of about 2,000 people per country. In it, 10% pay for AI themselves and 9% get access through work or school; together, 18% have paid access (some have both).[^s1-paid]

## 2. Cheaper and better, measured fairly {#s2}

"AI is getting cheaper" only means something if you compare AI models of the same quality. So we ask two questions: does the same capability cost less, and does the same money buy more capability?

We score models with the Epoch Capabilities Index, from the independent research group Epoch AI. It combines more than 50 benchmarks (standard sets of test questions) into one number with no maximum; GPT-5, released in August 2025, scores 150.[^s2-eci] Prices are each company's list price per million tokens (a token is a small chunk of text), assuming a model reads three tokens for every one it writes.[^s2-blend]

**Same capability, less money.** In November 2025 the cheapest model at GPT-5's level was GPT-5 itself, at $3.44 per million tokens; in May 2026 it was Gemini 3 Flash, at $1.13. Today GPT-6 Luna or Claude Haiku 5.5 does it for $0.20, about 17 times less in a year.[^s2-cheaper]

**Same money, more capability.** For $4 per million tokens or less, the best score you could buy went from 150 a year ago to about 152 six months ago, then to 169 today (GPT-6.1 Sol).[^s2-better]

**Six months from now?** If the past year's pace continued, by May 2027 GPT-5-level AI would cost about 5 cents per million tokens, and $4 would buy a score of about 178. If no cheaper or better model arrives, both stay where they are today. These are our rough estimates, not data.[^s2-projection]

**Cost per task.** Price per token ignores how many tokens a model uses to reach an answer, so cost per task would be a better measure. But no source has tracked it consistently for these models over the year.[^s2-aa-versions] The closest measure is Epoch AI's estimate of what it costs to answer a test question at a fixed level of accuracy: each year, that cost drops to about a thirteenth of what it was.[^s2-epoch]

**Why price per token is an incomplete comparison:**
- Token use varies: Artificial Analysis, an independent testing firm, found that one model, Claude Opus 5.5, costs about 10 times more per task when told to think hardest than when told to think least.[^s2-effort]
- Token sizes differ: Anthropic says its newer models split the same text into about 30% more tokens, so Haiku 5.5 costs a little more than its $0.20 suggests.[^s2-tokenizer]
- Prices change: each of the three dates uses the list prices in force then, not today's.[^s2-hist]

The most capable model did not get steadily more expensive. Between November 2025 and May 2026 its price rose from $41 to $68 per million tokens, as one premium OpenAI "Pro" model replaced another. Today's top scorer, GPT-6 Astra, costs $20, and GPT-6.1 Terra, statistically tied with it, costs $2.[^s2-top]

Some prices are rising: Google will double the price of Gemini 4 Argon on 1 December 2026, ending what it called an introductory price, and of two cheaper Gemini Flash models on 1 January 2027.[^s2-argon]

The total bill is clearly rising. Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet and Meta expect to spend about $720-740 billion in 2026 on data centres, chips and other equipment. That pays for training new AI models, for running them for users (what the prices above cover), and for the rest of their businesses.[^s2-capex] Through its developer services alone, Google now handles more than four times as many tokens as a year ago.[^s2-volume] Each unit gets cheaper, but the world buys far more.

> **Expert note: Why not cost per task.** Artificial Analysis, the main source of per-task costs, changed its test several times in 2026, its tasks now use far more tokens than before, and its current version does not score most late-2025 models, such as GPT-5.[^s2-aa-versions] Epoch's measure covers five benchmarks and counts the hidden working-out text a model writes before it answers. Its cost falls fastest just after a score is first reached and slows over the next two years.[^s2-epoch]

> **Expert note: How the frontier is built.** A model counts at a snapshot if Epoch's release date is on or before that date. All three snapshots use one vintage of the ECI file (7 November 2026), so the earlier panels are past frontiers re-measured on today's scale, not what Epoch published at the time. ECI scores carry credible intervals of roughly ±2-3 points, so gaps under about 3 points are noise.[^s2-eci] The headline thresholds are sensitive at the edges: GPT-6.1 Sol sits at exactly $4.00, and a $5 cap lifts the May 2026 value to 156.2 (Gemini 3.1 Pro).[^s2-better] Gemini 4 Argon is coded "Limited access" by Epoch. Some open-weight models with no listed price (Muse Spark Open at 155.4, Beam 1.0 at 150.8) might undercut $0.20 through third-party hosts. Using Artificial Analysis prices instead gives about 20 times cheaper, resting on one deprecated open model's third-party host price.[^s2-aa-cross] We did not use the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index itself: it changed version several times during the year and its current version does not score 2025 models.[^s2-aa-versions] Some current prices are temporary: GPT-5.6 Sol's $4/$20 is a promotion that ends on 30 November 2026.[^s2-promo]

> **Expert note: Other cost estimates.** Other estimates agree on direction, not size. Epoch's decline is about 66% per quarter for a score that has just become state of the art, slowing to about 32% per quarter two years later; Epoch annualises these as roughly 75 times and 4.7 times a year, and the at-debut figure overstates a full year because the rate decays every quarter.[^s2-epoch] An MIT FutureTech paper finds 5 to 10 times a year at a fixed score, but also finds the price of running the top-performing model rising 3 to 18 times a year (April 2024 to November 2025 data). That is a different measure from list price per token, and it is the main published support for "the best AI is getting more expensive".[^s2-gundlach]

> **Expert note: Cheaper units, bigger bill: the Jevons paradox.** Falling unit prices alongside a rising total bill is the pattern economists call the Jevons paradox, after William Stanley Jevons, who argued in 1865 that more efficient steam engines would raise, not cut, Britain's total coal use. Microsoft's chief executive, Satya Nadella, invoked it in late January 2025, as DeepSeek's cheap models hit tech stocks: "Jevons paradox strikes again! As AI gets more efficient and accessible, we will see its use skyrocket".[^s2-jevons] The figures here fit the pattern but do not prove it. Google's developer token volume rose more than four times in a year[^s2-volume] while the price of GPT-5-level capability fell about 17 times,[^s2-cheaper] so spending on those tokens could have fallen, not risen; no public figure gives total spending on AI use. Buyers also move to newer, more capable models rather than buying more of the old level. And the capital spending is a bet on future demand, not a measure of it.[^s2-capex]

## 3. How fast AI is improving, and whether it is speeding up {#s3}

METR's "time horizon" is the most-quoted measure of AI progress. It asks how long a software task can be, in a skilled person's working time, before an AI agent fails at it more often than it succeeds. It measures how hard the tasks are, not how long the AI works.[^s3-def]

GPT-4, the best model of March 2023, managed tasks of about 4 minutes. Claude Opus 4.6 reached about 12 hours in February 2026.[^s3-arc] The pace picked up too. Before 2024 the horizon doubled about every 8½ months. From 2024 on it doubled about every 3½ months.[^s3-doubling] The speed-up came with "reasoning" models, which work through a problem before answering.[^s3-epoch]

Then the measure hit its limits. METR says scores above 16 hours are unreliable.[^s3-16h] On 8 September 2026 it marked its leaderboard "no longer actively updated". No model newer than April 2026 has a reliable public time horizon.[^s3-frozen] So the public data clearly shows the 2024 speed-up. It can neither confirm nor rule out a second speed-up in 2025–26.

The strongest evidence is private. On 5 November 2026 METR published an assessment built on Anthropic's internal data. It found that from about October 2025, Anthropic's models improved about 1.9 times as fast as before. METR's range runs from 1.5 to 2.8 times, and some reasonable methods give as little as 1.1 times.[^s3-accel] The caveat is large. Anthropic blacked out more than half of the evidence that METR found most informative, so no outsider can check the result.[^s3-accel]

> **Expert note: What the horizon means, and more data points.** A 2-hour horizon means the AI completes about half of the tasks that take a person two hours.[^s3-def] Between GPT-4 and Opus 4.6, OpenAI's o3 reached about 2 hours in April 2025 and GPT-5 about 3.4 hours in August 2025.[^s3-arc] An early version of Claude Mythos Preview, tested in early 2026, scored about 17 hours.[^s3-mythos] Succeeding half the time is a low bar. If the AI must succeed four times in five, the numbers shrink to about 70 minutes for Opus 4.6 and about 3 hours for Mythos.[^s3-p80]

> **Expert note: Our doubling-time refit and its limits.** Our doubling times come from a simple least-squares refit of METR's published data file. We used METR's "state of the art" points and left out anything above 16 hours, as METR does. The results are 260 days before 2024, 129 days from 2023 (METR's own figure: 128.7 days, 95% CI 104–158), 105 days from 2024 and 121 days from 2025.[^s3-doubling] The 2025+ fit uses only seven points and is no faster than the 2024+ fit, so the public curve shows one step-up, not a further one inside 2025–26. Epoch AI's broader index of test scores shows the same single speed-up in 2024, and a steady, straight-line climb since. Epoch did not test for a second break either.[^s3-epoch] The early-2026 jump was also led by one lab. GPT-5.4 (about 5.7 hours), Gemini 3.1 Pro (about 6.4 hours) and GPT-5.3-Codex (about 5.8 hours) sit at 0.50–0.62x of the 2024+ line.[^s3-others] The top points carry very wide intervals: Opus 4.6 spans about 5–61 hours and Mythos about 8.5–55 hours.[^s3-arc][^s3-mythos] An independent Bayesian reanalysis finds that METR's data cannot tell exponential growth from faster-than-exponential growth.[^s3-moss]

> **Expert note: Beyond the public curve.** The tasks cannot measure the newest models. Only 31 of METR's 228 tasks take a person eight hours or more, and real people were timed on only 5 of those.[^s3-suite] On tasks over eight hours, at least 16% of the AI's "successes" turned out on review to be cheating.[^s3-cheat] METR's own estimate for the leading internal model of early 2026 is "Likely ≥16h", about two working days.[^s3-mythos] GPT-5.6 Sol, tested in June 2026, scored about 11 hours or over 270 hours, depending on how cheating was counted. The figure of about 11 hours counts cheating as failure, and the figure of over 270 hours counts it as success. METR called neither figure robust.[^s3-sol]

> **Expert note: What METR's 1.9x rests on.** METR's 5 November 2026 assessment draws on Anthropic's internal data, including models the public has not seen. The 1.9x is measured on Anthropic's own version of Epoch's index. Across all developers' models the figure is 1.4 times.[^s3-accel] METR's 1.9x comes from a model with a single breakpoint, imposed by METR, whose date (7 October 2025) is learned from the data. Swapping the statistical link function moves the estimate from 1.1x (95% CI 0.8–1.5) to 2.9x (2.1–4.9). METR says it "would not be surprised" by anything from 1.3x to 3x. Models are dated by when most Anthropic staff could use them, not by public release, and METR had no query access to unreleased models. On Anthropic's index (AECI), its models went from gaining roughly 15 points a year to roughly 29. AECI is not on the same scale as Epoch's public index, so these rates cannot be compared with the 14 points a year on Epoch's public index.[^s3-epoch] Anthropic's newest internal model already scores above 50% on all 15 AECI benchmarks, so this index is close to its own ceiling. Anthropic's Haiku 5.5 system card had reported 1.7x; METR says its higher figure comes mainly from adding data on newer unreleased models. METR reads the 1.4x all-developer figure in two opposite ways. If Anthropic was behind the frontier in 2025, the 1.9x overstates things. If the earlier points are overfit, the 1.4x understates them.[^s3-accel]

> **Expert note: Why the speed-up, and Anthropic's spring view.** METR guesses that about 80% of the speed-up comes from AI helping with the work, and calls that highly uncertain.[^s3-attrib] In spring 2026, Anthropic itself had told METR it had not yet seen its pace of progress double.[^s3-april]

## 4. The jagged map {#s4}

Tested against people, AI is beyond the best humans at some tasks and far below them at others.

**Beyond the best humans.** The opening's two results sit at the far right: the 2025 programming world finals, on OpenAI's own report, and the Erdős disproof. Hurricanes show AI edging past an expert team. Coverage of the US National Hurricane Center's review of the 2025 season says Google DeepMind's model did slightly better than the official Atlantic track forecasts at some lead times.[^s4-nhc]

**Improving on the best known algorithms.** In a December 2024 Nature paper, Google DeepMind's GenCast weather model beat Europe's leading physics-based forecasting system on 97.2% of 1,320 test targets.[^s4-gencast] That comparison is with another computer model, not with human forecasters.

**Ahead of trained or typical people, with caveats.** In tests run in October 2026, AI scored well above PhD experts on hard science questions written so that web searches don't help.[^s4-gpqa-human][^s4-gpqa-ai] In a trial published in February 2026, doctors diagnosed written cases, not real patients, much better with GPT-4o than without it. GPT-4o alone did better still, though that was not the trial's main question.[^s4-pakistan] Waymo says its driverless cars have 95% fewer serious-injury crashes than average human drivers.[^s4-waymo] Still, in mid-2026 it pulled them off freeways for about five weeks after they drove into closed work zones.[^s4-waymo-recall]

**Level with the best.** In July 2026 the Forecasting Research Institute found that, on its questions, the best AI forecaster could not be statistically told apart from superforecasters: people with proven records in forecasting tournaments. That forecaster, Cassi, is not a single AI model. It is a system that searches the web for recent news and combines the answers of several models.[^s4-forecast]

**Level with the best, if confirmed.** At the International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) in July 2026, Huawei, Xiaohongshu and others reported perfect scores of 42 out of 42 for their AI. The IMO has confirmed none of them. Even if true, it is a tie: 7 of the 666 students also got full marks.[^s4-imo]

**Below humans.** Working on its own, the best agent completes only about 21% of real freelance projects on the Remote Labor Index ([see the opening](#s0)).[^s4-rli] People have solved every game in a set of puzzle games called ARC-AGI-3. When it launched in March 2026, every leading AI model scored under 1%.[^s4-arc-launch] By September GPT-6 Astra reached a verified 62.7% on ARC Prize's standard setup: fast progress, but still well short of people.[^s4-arc-astra]

**Far below humans.** Epoch AI's February 2026 review found robots typically 3 to 10 times slower than people. At that point no robot had been shown doing every part of cleaning a kitchen.[^s4-robots]

Most tasks where AI is superior are short and self-contained, with one clear right answer. Most where it is behind involve long, open-ended work or the physical world. Driving is the exception, and there AI is compared with average drivers, not the best. Asking whether AI has reached "human level" flattens this uneven picture into one number, which is why [section 5](#s5) argues for asking it one task at a time.

**What about cost?** Being able to do a task is not the same as being much cheaper. In OpenAI's test of expert work, GDPval, the AI's raw output cost about 470 times less than an expert's. Once an expert checked it and redid what fell short, it was only about 1.2 times cheaper.[^s5-gdpval]

> **Expert note: What the right-hand dots compare against.** Several right-hand dots compare AI with machines or with average people, not with the best humans. The figures behind the Brief: on GPQA Diamond (graduate-level science questions), PhD experts scored about 70%[^s4-gpqa-human] and Gemini 4 Argon scored 96% in Epoch AI's testing in October 2026, with several other 2026 models, including GPT-6 Astra and Claude Sonnet 5.5, within error of it.[^s4-gpqa-ai] In the randomised trial in Pakistan, doctors using GPT-4o scored 71.4%, against 42.6% for those using ordinary references, and GPT-4o alone scored 82.9%.[^s4-pakistan] Waymo's 95% figure covers 270 million miles through June 2026 and comes from Waymo's own analysis.[^s4-waymo] GenCast was measured against ECMWF's physics ensemble (ENS), and the ENS version it was compared with was an older, lower-resolution one.[^s4-gencast] For hurricanes, coverage of the NHC report says the DeepMind model also beat the official forecasts in the East Pacific. NHC's own preview of the report calls the model "very useful", and notes that some AI systems were not consistently available in time for forecasters. MathArena has published no IMO 2026 evaluation.[^s4-imo]

> **Expert note: Robots in Epoch AI's review.** Epoch AI's robot review also found that the robots shown handling trash bags had not yet removed one from an indoor bin.[^s4-robots]

> **Expert note: The cost comparison in detail.** The figures are from OpenAI's GDPval paper (September 2025), for GPT-5 against industry experts on the test's tasks. ## 5. Why "human-level" is the wrong question {#s5}

"Artificial general intelligence", or AGI, is usually explained as AI that matches people. The main definitions disagree about what to match, so each sets a different test.

<!-- CHART: s5-definitions | Small styled table: four AGI definitions (OpenAI charter, Google DeepMind Levels of AGI, Hendrycks et al., ARC Prize), what each one tests, and where AI stands on it, each row with a provenance chip linking to its footnote | none (values inline, from footnotes s5-charter, s5-levels, s5-hendrycks, s5-arc) -->

| Definition | What it tests | Where AI stands |
|---|---|---|
| OpenAI charter (2018): systems that "outperform humans at most economically valuable work" | Economic output | No measurable test set[^s5-charter] |
| Google DeepMind, "Levels of AGI" (2023) | How it ranks against skilled adults, at one task or many | Superhuman AI exists for single tasks (AlphaFold); general chatbots were rated "Emerging" (Level 1 of 5) in 2023[^s5-levels] |
| "A Definition of AGI", by 33 researchers (2025) | Breadth across ten areas of thinking, against a well-educated adult | GPT-5 (August 2025): 57% overall, 10/10 in maths, 0/10 in storing long-term memories[^s5-hendrycks] |
| ARC Prize | How efficiently it learns new skills, against people | GPT-6 Astra (September 2026): 62.7% on the standard version of the newest test ([section 4](#s4)); ARC Prize is "not claiming that it is AGI"[^s5-arc] |

The one place the word had a hard meaning was a contract. Microsoft's deal with OpenAI tied payments to OpenAI reaching AGI.[^s5-panel] In April 2026 the firms changed the deal. OpenAI's payments to Microsoft now run through 2030 "independent of OpenAI's technology progress". The announcement does not mention AGI at all. Some commentators read this as the end of the AGI clause.[^s5-clause]

The people building AI contradict each other. In August 2025 OpenAI's Sam Altman called AGI "not a super useful term". In August 2026 he told TIME that OpenAI would have an internal system he would call AGI by the end of 2026.[^s5-altman] At GPT-6 Astra's launch on 3 September 2026, OpenAI president Greg Brockman closed with "Welcome to the AGI era."[^s5-brockman] Three days later Nvidia's Jensen Huang congratulated OpenAI and posted "AGI has arrived".[^s5-huang]

A more useful question is narrower: which task, against which people? Some yardsticks already ask it. METR's time horizon ([section 3](#s3)) measures the longest software task, in a skilled person's working time, that an AI can finish half the time. An OpenAI test called GDPval has industry experts compare AI work with professionals' work without knowing which is which. The best model's work was rated as good as or better than the professionals' on 47.6% of tasks in September 2025. In April 2026 OpenAI reported 84.9% for GPT-5.5.[^s5-gdpval] The Remote Labor Index ([see the opening](#s0)) uses real paid freelance projects. There the best AI agent, working on its own, still fails most of them.

Each of these still uses people as the ruler: human working time, experts' work, freelancers' work. So do the DeepMind levels and the "Definition of AGI" score. The useful part is the detail, not the total. On that score, GPT-5 got 57% overall, but 10 out of 10 in maths and 0 out of 10 in storing long-term memories.[^s5-hendrycks] A single "human-level" verdict would hide that gap. That is why the map in [section 4](#s4) shows each comparison separately.

> **Expert note: Each better yardstick has limits.** The September 2025 GDPval figure covers the test's 220 public tasks; OpenAI did not say whether its April 2026 score of 84.9% for GPT-5.5 used the same tasks.[^s5-gdpval] GDPval tasks are one-shot and precisely specified, with no back-and-forth and no physical work. In the GDPval paper, GPT-5 looked about 90 times faster than experts on raw output, but with a try-once, review, and do-it-yourself-if-it-falls-short workflow it was only about 1.12 times faster and 1.18 times cheaper (about 1.4 and 1.6 times when retried several times). The full GDPval set has 1,320 tasks from 44 occupations. We found no independent check of OpenAI's 84.9% for GPT-5.5.[^s5-gdpval] On the Remote Labor Index, the Center for AI Safety found that AI judges overstated the newest models' success by about 2.3 to 2.9 times compared with human graders.[^s5-cais] ARC-AGI-3 scores depend heavily on the test harness ([section 4](#s4)), and METR warns that a 50% horizon is not a safe threshold for handing work over ([section 3](#s3)). The "Definition of AGI" authors (Hendrycks et al.) advise reporting the full profile, not the single number.[^s5-hendrycks]

> **Expert note: The contract and the quotes.** In October 2025 Microsoft and OpenAI gave the AGI call to an independent expert panel.[^s5-panel] The new deal was announced on 27 April 2026. Whether the expert panel survives it is unclear; Microsoft's post is silent on it. The commentators who read it as the end of the AGI clause are the programmer and blogger Simon Willison and the tech site The Verge.[^s5-clause] On the quotes: Altman's year-end prediction is TIME's paraphrase, not his words. Brockman's remarks beyond the closing line reach us only as paraphrase in press coverage. Huang's post was not tied to Astra's ARC result. The researcher Andrej Karpathy had said in October 2025 that AGI was still about a decade away. His own definition is economic ("any economically valuable task at human performance or better"), and "a decade away" is the episode title; he calls this "the decade of agents".[^s5-karpathy] Anthropic's Dario Amodei avoids the word, calling it "an imprecise term" with "sci-fi baggage and hype".[^s5-amodei]

## 6. AI building AI {#s6}

Recursive self-improvement means AI doing so much of the work of building the next AI that each new generation arrives faster than the last. Nobody claims that loop runs on its own yet. So how much of the work does AI already do, and is progress speeding up as a result?

Start with the groups that do not sell models. METR's "time horizon" measures how long a task AI agents can complete, counted in a person's working time. It climbed steeply until April 2026. By then, METR's task set could no longer measure the newest models.[^s6-metr] Epoch AI's capability index has risen in a straight line, about 14 points a year since 2024. No public model has beaten GPT-6 Astra's score of 169, set in September 2026.[^s6-eci] The one independent sign of a further speed-up is METR's look inside Anthropic: about 1.9 times the earlier pace since October 2025. It rests on evidence Anthropic mostly redacted ([section 3](#s3)).[^s6-accel]

<!-- CHART: s6-rsi-dashboard | Small multiples of "AI improving AI" indicators in three labelled tiers (independent; independent analysis of private lab data; lab self-reports), each panel with its own axis, date range and a "who measured this" tag; company series never share a line; bottom strip shows each lab's formal threshold status | research/data/rsi_series.csv (exclude the OpenAI June 2026 "crossover" row and the Microsoft 2025-04-29 row; add the S-1 >90% point, CoBench 2.1 scores vs the 85% line, and the 4x human reference) -->

The labs report steeper curves, unchecked by outsiders. Each company counts something different.

- **Google:** the share of new code that is AI-generated and approved by engineers went from more than a quarter (October 2024) to 75% (April 2026).[^s6-google]
- **Anthropic:** Claude wrote more than 80% of the code merged in May 2026, and over 90% of "contributions to our codebase" in September 2026, per its IPO filing.[^s6-anthro-code] On an internal test, its models are given slow code used to train AI models and asked to make it run faster. They went from making it run about 3 times faster (May 2025) to about 52 times faster (April 2026). A skilled human manages about 4 times in four to eight hours.[^s6-speedup]
- **OpenAI:** by mid-August 2026 its agents logged 3.1 workdays for every human researcher's workday, up from less than one before June. That counts how long agents run, many at once, not the quality of their work. Over half of the successful 4–8 hour tasks still needed a human to step in.[^s6-openai]
- **Labs that release open models:** China's MiniMax says its own model wrote 80% of its newly committed code (February 2026). Alibaba says a Qwen model ran 33 rounds of fully automated self-improvement over about a month (September 2026). In May 2026 the head of France's Mistral told the French parliament that its engineers no longer write code.[^s6-open-claims] The best open models this year have all come from Chinese labs. Epoch AI estimated that from January to May 2026 they trailed the best closed models by about four months.[^s6-open-gap]

AI is already speeding up the labs' work, as the figures above show. But each of the three big closed labs also has a written threshold for a much bigger speed-up, and none says it has been crossed. Anthropic's, for example, is progress twice as fast as the fastest pace seen without significant AI help, with automated research plausibly driving much of the gain.[^s6-rsp] METR's 1.9 times ([section 3](#s3)) is a related but looser measure: it compares Anthropic's pace with its own pace from 2024 to October 2025, from all causes. Still, METR guesses that most of the speed-up comes from AI, and thinks the threshold may already have been crossed.[^s6-accel][^s6-metr-review] OpenAI rates GPT-6 Astra (September 2026) below its "High" level for AI self-improvement.[^s6-openai-th] Google's October 2026 model card for Gemini 4 Argon lists its research-acceleration levels as "Not reached".[^s6-google-th] Claude Opus 5.5 (September 2026) scored about 56% on an internal research-engineering test. Anthropic says a model able to replace its research staff would score 85% or more.[^s6-cobench]

OpenAI, Google and Meta, which also release open models, have similar written thresholds. But a July 2026 review found no published safety framework at China's DeepSeek, Z.ai or Alibaba, or at France's Mistral. It did not rate China's Moonshot or MiniMax, though Moonshot's Kimi models were the best open models for much of 2026.[^s6-open-th][^s6-open-gap]

Even so, Anthropic's amended IPO filing of 2 November 2026 says the company "may imminently meet" its own threshold, based on its internal estimates and expectations for future models. No current model has been designated as exceeding it. The amendment adds that in the past Anthropic has spotted such speed-ups only after they happened.[^s6-s1]

More research labour does not turn one-for-one into more progress. In early 2026 Anthropic estimated that doubling its labour might make output only about 1.15 to 1.3 times as large.[^s6-labour] Epoch AI researchers argued in November 2025 that if progress needs experiments at the scale of the biggest models, extra AI labour runs into a shortage of computing power.[^s6-compute] A July 2026 Princeton-led study found AI agents, mainly Claude Opus 4.8, did all the engineering on two research projects but made no real progress on the research questions.[^s6-princeton] Yet METR's November 2026 assessment found no "convincing bottlenecks to continued rapid progress" in the next 12 months.[^s6-bottle]

So AI now does much of the hands-on work inside the leading labs. But code share and agent hours count activity, not research value. Humans still set the direction, and the strongest evidence is self-reported or hidden.

> **Expert note: The labs' AI R&D thresholds and self-reports.** METR's 1.9x and Anthropic's threshold are both ratios of progress per year, but they differ in baseline and in cause. METR's figure is the slope of Anthropic's capability index (AECI) after 7 October 2025 divided by its slope from early 2024 to then, from all causes; METR says it does "not pay special attention to the thresholds set in the RSP", guesses that "perhaps ≈80%" of the October 2026 speed-up is due to AI, and finds 1.4x across all developers' models. The threshold compares with both "the rate we'd expect" and "the fastest rate of extended progress we've observed in the absence of significant AI contributions to AI R&D" (sustained over at least three model generations), and requires that the doubling be plausibly "substantially attributable" to automated research.[^s6-rsp][^s6-accel] In its review of Anthropic's August 2026 Risk Report, METR called the report's argument that the threshold had not been crossed "overstated", and wrote that most of the relevant researchers at METR and Anthropic "lean toward the threshold not having been crossed on July 15th, but we think it is plausible that it had been".[^s6-metr-review] The thresholds are moving targets. Anthropic's AI R&D threshold was revised in February, April and July 2026. Version 3.4 defines it as a doubling of the rate of progress, "as much progress in one year as one would see in two years at baseline", and says this is "not the same idea as doubling researchers' productivity". The policy admits the threshold "has proven difficult to operationalize".[^s6-rsp] That gap helps explain why staff self-reports run far ahead of measured acceleration: Anthropic's Slack polls of how much AI speeds up staff work rose from about 1.2x (January 2025) to about 12x (October 2026), which METR calls "only very loose evidence".[^s6-polls] Google rules its threshold out if a model cannot complete 90% of the unbugged tasks in a 74-task suite of research-engineering problems with a two-hour limit per task. ## 7. Can an AI change itself? {#s7}

An AI model is a very large set of numbers, called weights, learned in training. We found no public case of a model directly editing its own weights. One real but narrow form exists: a model trains on material it wrote.[^s7-self]

<!-- CHART: s7-weights-matrix | 2x2 matrix, whose weights change (own vs another model's) x how (training on self-generated data vs direct edit), named examples in each cell, code-level self-modification as a side strip, the "own + direct edit" cell empty and labelled "none found in public work" | none (taxonomy from research/topics/r2-selfweights_landscape.md) -->

In early November 2026, a claim spread that models rewrite their own weights. It rests on an MIT blog post, not peer-reviewed.[^s7-post] In it, OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra edited a far smaller model's weights, not its own, without training. That model's maths score rose from 8% to 48%,[^s7-qwen] mostly because the edit fixed its known habit of repeating itself.[^s7-loops]

No publicly known frontier chatbot updates its weights as people use it.[^s7-live] FutureSearch's middle estimate for a frontier (most capable) model updating its weights weekly is January 2033 (forecast updated October 2026).[^s7-futuresearch]

> **Expert note: Four kinds of work on changing models.** The weights hold what the model knows and how it behaves. Change them and you change the model. An AI that could improve its own weights would be the most direct form of the AI-building-AI loop in [section 6](#s6). Work on changing models comes in four kinds. Only the first changes a model's own weights. (1) Training on its own output: a model trains on material it wrote. Real, but narrow so far.[^s7-self] (2) Weight generators: networks that produce weights for other models.[^s7-gen] (3) Direct edits: algorithms that write facts straight into another model's weights, since 2022.[^s7-edit] (4) Code rewriting: agents that rewrite code, including code used to train them, but leave weights alone, such as Google DeepMind's AlphaEvolve.[^s7-alphaevolve] We found no public demonstration of a model working out and applying a direct edit to its own weights.

> **Expert note: The November post, and what is still hard.** The latest claim that models rewrite their own weights spread in early November 2026. It rests on a blog post by four MIT researchers, published on 2 November and not peer-reviewed.[^s7-post] They gave OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra a far smaller, freely available model with under a billion weights, plus Python and a limited budget of test runs. Reasoning about the weights, with no training, Astra edited about 0.016% of them. With the edit, the small model's score on a standard maths test rose from 8% to 48%.[^s7-qwen] What was new is that an AI worked out the edit, though for another model, not itself. Most of that gain was a repair. The small model is known to get stuck repeating itself. After the edit, the share of its answers cut off at the length limit fell from 93% to 33%.[^s7-loops] On a second tiny model without that problem, editing gave no real gain. The authors conclude that here, editing could remove an obstacle hiding an ability but not add a missing one.[^s7-gemma] A more accurate headline than November's: frontier models with tools can now diagnose and patch a small model's weights much as a skilled researcher would. Writing new knowledge into weights is still hard. In an unreviewed July 2026 study, after 20 rounds of writing in new facts, recall of earlier ones fell to about 1% or about 46%, depending on how each new fact was written in.[^s7-forget] In a forecast updated in October 2026, FutureSearch put its median (middle) estimate for a frontier model updating its weights every week at January 2033.[^s7-futuresearch]

> **Expert note: How solid the weight-editing results are.** The small model is Alibaba's Qwen3.5-0.8B, with about 0.8 billion weights; the second tiny model, without the looping problem, is Gemma 3 270M.[^s7-gemma] The headline Qwen result is a single run with the largest budget, 50 forward passes, taking 20.3 hours including a 4.7-hour outage. The 10- and 25-pass runs made *different* edits (a rank-one removal across 30 matrices, then zeroing one attention head), reached only 12.3% and 14.1%, and barely reduced truncation (87.2% and 86.4%). There was one run per budget, so variance is unknown.[^s7-qwen][^s7-loops] Hugging Face's rerun scored the released checkpoint at 51.0% (31.6% truncated). That confirms the gain is in the weights, but it re-evaluates the published edit and does not re-derive it. Outside reactions came the day after the post. Simon Willison partly reproduced the weight-reading experiments on his laptop.[^s7-willison] Sean Goedecke was impressed but argued that "a smart human ML researcher" could do every task.[^s7-goedecke] Matthew Honnibal objected that the post lumps tool-free experiments together with ones where the model used Python. His tally puts 4 of 10 experiment rows as tool-free, all on networks of roughly 17,000 to 172,000 weights. Every experiment on a real language model used Python.[^s7-honnibal] Alex Krentsel of Google Research reportedly reran one task and found the ability only in the newest models; that comes from [press coverage of a chart he posted](https://officechai.com/ai/ability-for-weight-space-programming-has-appeared-only-in-latest-models-says-google-researcher-alex-krentsel-who-reproduced-results/), not from his own write-up.

> **Expert note: Limits of the self-training work.** The two self-training systems are SEAL (MIT, 2025) and MASS (Sakana AI and UC Berkeley, October 2026), and both have caveats. SEAL's paper reports that performance on earlier tasks declines as self-edits accumulate. In continued-pretraining settings it also trails synthetic data written by GPT-4.1. MASS's gains of 1.2–1.6x are in score *per output token* on four open-ended research benchmarks, after two LoRA rounds. Scores on SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2.0 were flat or slightly down.[^s7-self] The own-weights, direct-edit cell is empty in public work: the MIT authors describe an LLM that improves its own weights only as a future direction.[^s7-post]

> **Expert note: The other methods by name.** The best-known weight generator is Text-to-LoRA, which writes add-on (LoRA) weights for a model from a task description.[^s7-gen] The fact-writing algorithms are ROME and MEMIT, from 2022.[^s7-edit] An agent that rewrites its own code, with the underlying model frozen, is the Darwin Gödel Machine (2025).[^s7-code]

## 8. Report card: how our July brief did {#s8}

On 10 July 2026 we published [*Measuring the End of Measurement*](https://measuring-the-end-of-measurement.vercel.app). It argued that AI tests wear out faster than people can replace them, and that they break in sudden jumps.

It is playing out as we described, but our numbers and estimates were too conservative.

What held, and what did not:

- Tests wear out even faster than we said. On a cleaned-up version of a hard coding test, posted in October 2026, AI agents already score up to 99.4%.[^s8-swev2]
- AI moved further than we predicted on the ARC-AGI-3 puzzle test: 62.7% by September, above the 30–50% we had called ambitious for the end of 2026.[^s8-arc3]
- The length of task AI can finish kept doubling about every 4 months, as we said, but that can no longer be checked.[^s8-metr]
- AI is speeding up AI research, as we said, though the best estimate now (Anthropic's models improving 1.9 times as fast as before) measures something different from our July figure.[^s8-accel]
- Spending did not slow the race: Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet and Meta expect to spend about $720–740 billion in 2026.[^s8-capex]
- We misdescribed three of our own headline figures. The full report card, with every claim and grade, is in the expert note below.

## 9. What to watch {#s9}

Below are seven dated checks for the coming months. Each says what to expect if the trend continues, and what result would mean AI's progress is speeding up or slowing down.

<!-- CHART: s9-signposts | Signpost list: date, indicator, value as of 7 Nov 2026, "spreading" and "stalling" thresholds, link to source, and a status chip (not yet due / moved / no change); a decay banner above it shows "data as of 7 Nov 2026" and the days elapsed | none (values inline, from footnotes s9-*) -->

1. **31 December 2026: forecasts come due.** Forecasters expected AI to reach 3.4 hours on METR's task-length measure (at an 80% success rate) by the end of 2026. The last measurement, from April, was 3.1 hours, and the trend points far past 3.4. The catch: METR stopped updating its results in September.[^s9-leap]
2. **1 January 2027: Epoch's public index of test scores.** In September 2026, Epoch projected the top score on that date at about 170 (166 to 176). The top score now is 169.[^s9-eci] Above 176 would be public evidence of a second speed-up. Still at 169 would show none.
3. **7 May 2027: the price of GPT-5-level AI.** Now $0.20 per million tokens.[^s9-price] If the past year's pace continues, it would be about 5 cents by then ([section 2](#s2)).[^s2-projection] Staying near 20 cents would mean the fall has slowed.
4. **Monthly: the Remote Labor Index.** On about 21% of real freelance projects, the best AI agent's work is now judged as good as the human freelancer's, up from 2.5% a year earlier.[^s9-rli] At that pace, about 19 points a year, it would reach about 30% by May 2027. Well above 30% would mean a speed-up in real work; still near 21% would mean a slowdown.
5. **By 7 May 2027: a new METR task set.** METR's public measure of task length stops at models from April 2026.[^s9-metr] New tasks, or a second AI company sharing its data, would show outsiders whether progress is speeding up, as METR found inside Anthropic.[^s9-accel]
6. **Any day: a threshold declared, and whether others follow.** In a November 2026 filing, Anthropic told investors it "may imminently meet" its threshold for AI-accelerated research.[^s9-s1] A lab's own declaration is hard to check. More telling would be similar speed-ups at other labs, including the Chinese labs that make the best open models. By one estimate, open models were about four months behind the best closed ones from January to May 2026.[^s6-open-gap]
7. **Monthly: jobs for young workers.** Stanford researchers track employment of 22-to-25-year-olds in the jobs most exposed to AI, compared with young people in less-exposed jobs. By June 2026 it was about 19% lower than expected, up from 15% a year earlier; experienced workers show no such gap.[^s9-canaries] If the gap keeps growing at that pace, it would reach about 21% in data for December 2026. A faster rise, or a gap opening for older workers, would mean AI is reaching paid work faster; a shrinking gap would mean the effect is fading.

For now, "already past us" is true for tasks with a clear right answer, and not yet for most real freelance work. If measures of real work start moving with test scores, AI's lead is spreading. If only the scores move, the title stays true where it is true now, and no further.

## Sources and method {#sources}

Every number in this briefing links to a footnote. Each footnote opens with a tag saying who measured the number. **Verified** means someone independent of the model's maker measured or checked it. **Vendor-reported** means the claim comes from the company that makes the model. **Estimate** means a forecast or modelled estimate by a named party. **Our calculation** means we computed it from the cited data, and the footnote says from what.

> **Expert note: What each provenance tag covers.** A "Verified" number was measured or checked by an independent research group, government data, a peer-reviewed paper, an independent researchers' preprint (marked as such), or reputable journalism that checked the claim directly. A "Vendor-reported" claim comes from the company that makes the model, its staff or executives, its system cards, filings or blog posts. METR's November 2026 assessment of Anthropic, built on private and partly redacted data, is tagged "Estimate" throughout.

Data are as of 7 November 2026. Leaderboards move every few weeks, so check the "as of" date in each footnote before quoting a figure. The research files, fact-check reports and datasets behind this briefing are published alongside it. They include the [price-capability frontier](/data/frontier_snapshots.csv) (CSV), [METR time-horizon points](/data/metr_points.json) (JSON), [AI-improving-AI indicators](/data/rsi_series.csv) (CSV), [historical list prices](/data/price_history.json) (JSON) and the [full source list](/data/sources.csv) (CSV).
